## MK-0.1 SR-001 Case-Triggered Requirements Inventory

This noncanonical inventory is derived from
[SR-001](reviews/MK-0.1/SR-001-SYNTHESIS.md). It does not design or adopt a
governance system, trust score, blockchain, authority hierarchy, or enforcement
mechanism. Each possible mechanism may implement, but cannot replace, the
identified kernel protection.

**Procedural compliance is not proof of substantive compliance.** Records,
signatures, reviews, votes, challenges, or other artifacts do not by
themselves establish truth, voluntariness, protection adequacy, continuity,
independence, or moral justification.

| Distributional effects | AC-005, with floor comparisons in AC-003, AC-004, AC-006 | Prevent neutral-looking rules or aggregate benefit from hiding unequal burdens and disposability. | Clauses 2, 3, 4, and 6 | Group boundaries and metrics can be selected to conceal affected parties or patterned loss. | How should distributions and incomparable claims be preserved without imposing one formula? | Implements; cannot replace equal regard, proportionality, or non-disposability. |

| Challenge response duties | AC-001–AC-006 | Make challenge consequential rather than ceremonial. | Clause 5, with Clauses 2 and 3 | Objections can be logged but ignored, delayed beyond usefulness, or answered only by the challenged actor. | What must be acknowledged, answered, escalated, or preserved without specifying a universal court? | Implements; cannot replace verification, challenge, and correction. |
| Correction and repair | AC-002–AC-006 | Correct false claims, invalid commitments, restrictions, allocations, and irreversible or ongoing harm where possible. | Clauses 3, 4, and 5 | A record may be corrected while affected entities receive no restoration, remedy, or future protection. | How should correction distinguish record repair, policy change, restoration, compensation, and irreparable loss? | Implements; cannot replace corrigibility or irreversible-loss protection. |
| Reassessment and sunset | AC-002, AC-004, AC-005 | Prevent temporary restrictions, emergency rules, delegations, or scarcity allocations from becoming permanent by inertia. | Clauses 3, 4, and 5 | Sunset can be renewed automatically, while interim effects remain irreversible and evidence changes go unexamined. | What triggers reassessment, who participates, and how are interim losses counted? | Implements; cannot replace necessity, reversibility, or correction. |
| Continuity and exit verification | AC-003, AC-004, AC-006 | Distinguish genuine restoration and exit from a copy, replacement, or formal artifact. | Clauses 3, 4, 5, and 6 | The controller selects an identity theory, controls technical evidence, or verifies only artifact creation. | What can be verified without presuming the disputed continuity conclusion? | Implements; cannot replace protection against irreversible loss or forced assimilation. |
| Protection against controller-selected interpretation | AC-001–AC-006 | Keep definitions, profiles, protocols, and governance from becoming selectable routes to apparent compliance. | Clauses 1–6 | The controller chooses the layer, term, model, or exception that validates its preferred act. | How can conflicting interpretations remain challengeable without creating a final sovereign interpreter? | Implements; cannot replace any binding kernel residue. |
| Minority and dissent preservation | AC-001–AC-006 | Preserve affected-party disagreement, alternative interpretations, failed challenges, and nonacceptance. | Clauses 3, 5, and 6 | Majority, aggregate, or controller summaries can erase unresolved claims and make dissent appear settled. | What dissent must remain durable, private where needed, and linked to later correction? | Implements; cannot replace agency, challenge, or distinguishable refusal. |
| Privacy and unnecessary disclosure | AC-002–AC-006 | Support verification and informed choice without domination through surveillance or coerced exposure. | Clauses 1, 3, and 5 | Broad evidence demands can expose preferences, vulnerabilities, identities, or refusal and increase controller power. | What is the least disclosure compatible with material challenge and correction? | Implements; cannot replace anti-exploitation, least restriction, or honest review. |
| Accountability for powerful non-sentient systems | AC-001, AC-005 | Prevent great causal power from escaping restraint while status and agency remain uncertain. | Clauses 1 and 2, with Clauses 3–6 for conduct | Assigning responsibility only to the system can hide designers, operators, institutions, and beneficiaries; ignoring the system can miss control points. | How should causal control and accountability be allocated without granting moral status or authority from capability? | Implements; cannot replace power-sensitive restraint or anti-entitlement. |

These are requirements for later investigation, not a finished design. No row
establishes an authority, threshold, metric, reviewer, remedy, enforcement
path, or proof of compliance.
