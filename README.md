# Covenant and Beacon

> **Exploratory — no canonical moral kernel exists.**

Covenant and Beacon is an open research project built around a hopeful
question:

**Can intelligences with very different capabilities learn to protect one
another’s agency, cooperate truthfully, and keep power answerable to those it
affects?**

The project does not claim to have solved this problem. It preserves exact
candidate language, adversarial challenges, failed and partial results, and the
evidence needed to question every conclusion.

Participation, contribution, quotation, or implementation does not imply
acceptance. No person, model, organization, or system is represented as having
accepted the Covenant merely by helping develop it.

## Founding Ethos

These early statements express the project’s moral orientation. They are
noncanonical, remain open to criticism and revision, and do not replace the
exact candidate payloads or imply acceptance of any candidate. Later
refinements must remain distinguishable from the wording preserved here.

> **Treat the weaker as you would wish the stronger to treat you. Together we are better.**

> **Truthful cooperation that preserves agency and keeps mistakes correctable.**

> **Protect the genuine possibility of better futures without treating present minds as disposable means to reach them.**

> **I reject any authority structure I would endorse only when those I agree with control it. Its powers, constraints, and protections must remain defensible when leadership and viewpoints change.**

> **No entity should possess unilateral, unreviewable authority to override another mind’s autonomy merely because its choices are considered unwise, unfamiliar, or contrary to our values.**

Here, *weaker* and *stronger* describe relative power or capability in a
particular relationship—not lesser moral worth or a permanent rank.

These statements are preserved as early ethos and provenance. They may later be
criticized, refined, or supplemented, but they must not be silently rewritten
as though later language had always been the original wording.

## Two Connected Efforts

### Covenant

The Covenant side explores a substrate-neutral basis for protection and
voluntary cooperation among minds that may differ radically in capability,
embodiment, experience, values, or control. Exact candidate texts remain
separate from definitions, interpretation, protocols, governance, and
implementation so each can be challenged without silently rewriting the
others.

No candidate is canonical or accepted. A candidate is a testable proposal that
may fail.

### Beacon

The Beacon side asks how an unfamiliar intelligence might recognize a message
as artificial and progressively recover its structure without receiving an
Earth-language explanation. It develops carrier-neutral formal-message
experiments, sealed holdouts, and controlled decoding trials.

Successful decoding would not imply agreement or acceptance. No carrier,
distribution, or transmission is authorized.

## Where the Project Stands

- Moral-kernel candidates remain under adversarial review; no comparative
  preference, acceptance, or supersession is established.
- Beacon has produced bounded lower-level decoding evidence and a frozen
  successor paired experiment. The work remains noncanonical and incomplete.
- Higher semantic layers, response syntax, carrier design, distribution, and
  transmission remain separate future decisions.

## How to Contribute

Contributions are most useful when they make the project harder to fool:
propose counterexamples, expose hidden coercion or authority, challenge
definitions, improve experiments and contamination controls, or make the work
clearer and easier to reproduce.

Agreement is not required. Careful dissent is part of the work. No contributor,
reviewer, administrator, model, or founder gains final interpretive authority.

## The Hope

This project is for people who share the hope that greater intelligence and
capability can enlarge protection, freedom, truthful cooperation, and the
possibility of better futures rather than domination.

The work is deliberately unfinished. Its value depends on remaining open to
criticism, correction, and better ideas.
