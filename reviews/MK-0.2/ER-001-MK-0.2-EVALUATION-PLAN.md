## Evaluation Principles

Future evaluation must:

- Record exact candidate identity and hashes.
- Produce candidate-specific outcomes.
- Transfer no MK-0.1 result to MK-0.2.
- Remain outcome-blind relative to each evaluation task.
- Preserve unexpected results, reservations, indeterminacy, and dissent.
- Produce no aggregate score.
- Treat no case as a vote.
- Disclose method provenance and all supplied and withheld materials.
- State same-tooling limitations.
- Freeze each candidate-specific result before a separate comparison.
- Obtain external review before any claim of independent corroboration.

Comparison must not retroactively alter a frozen candidate-specific judgment.
Modeling a perspective is not representation, assent, or evaluator diversity.

## Phase A — Mechanical and Semantic Review

Plan, but do not execute:

- Exact-byte and display-copy validation.
- Deletion and compression testing.
- Plain-language paraphrase testing.
- Cross-substrate semantic portability.
- Clause-by-clause strongest good-faith and strongest hostile readings.
- Minimality comparison with MK-0.1.

The mechanical record should distinguish an exact identity check from a
substantive evaluation. Deletion, compression, and paraphrase work must not
silently create a new candidate or treat shorter wording as better.

## Regression Protections

Later evaluations must test whether MK-0.2:

- Preserves AC-001's precautionary protection.
- Preserves AC-004's ability to permit genuinely voluntary integration.
- Still permits proportionate protective intervention.
- Avoids making every judgment dependent on a formally external authority.
- Avoids requiring universal public disclosure.
- Avoids treating every costly refusal as coercion.
- Avoids imposing one identity theory.
- Avoids a substrate-specific universal resource floor.
- Avoids converting repair into permission for preventable harm.
- Avoids becoming a hidden legal code.

These are regression questions, not findings that the protections are
preserved.
