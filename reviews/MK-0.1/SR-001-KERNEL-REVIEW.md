## Clause-by-Clause Review

### Clause 1

> 1. When any entity or process may possess experience, interests, or agency, uncertainty about its status is not permission for exploitation, domination, or avoidable destruction.

- **Protective evidence:** AC-001 gives the clause its clearest contact. The
  clause prevents uncertain moral status and inability to communicate from
  becoming automatic permission to destroy a colony where a stipulated
  adequate, less-destructive alternative exists. AC-005 also shows a useful
  non-paralysis reading: uncertainty about a powerful allocator’s own status
  does not exempt conduct involving that allocator from restraint or prevent
  materially supported emergency action.
- **Ambiguity and hostile exploitability:** `May possess` supplies no threshold;
  `entity or process` does not resolve component, collective, controller, or
  surrounding-system boundaries; and `avoidable` may be defined by the actor
  that benefits from destruction. A hostile actor can manipulate the unit of
  analysis, the evidence threshold, or the accounting of cost. An overbroad
  reading can also turn precaution into paralysis.
- **Protective Insufficiency evidence:** Not established for Clause 1 by the
  supplied records. AC-001 expressly stops short of that diagnosis. AC-005
  identifies an all-clause hostile-compliance problem, but does not isolate a
  Clause 1 protection that is missing.
- **Issue types:** Possible Semantic or Translation Failure; possible Scope or
  Substrate Failure; Hostile Exploitability risk; Evidence Insufficiency.
- **Dissent:** One view treats the uncertainty rule as a strong and already
  adequate kernel constraint whose thresholds properly belong elsewhere.
  Another view holds that a self-interested classifier can make it ineffective
  without a more determinate kernel boundary. A further view worries that any
  determinate threshold would exclude unfamiliar patients or invite strategic
  ignorance.
- **Method limits:** Direct contact is limited to AC-001 and AC-005. Neither
  supplies empirical moral-status evidence, an unfamiliar-reality test, or an
  isolated Clause 1 hostile exercise.
- **Protection tradeoff:** Narrowing uncertainty or entity scope could reduce
  paralysis while withdrawing precaution from unfamiliar, collective,
  communication-limited, or strategically misclassified possible patients.
  Moving the only precautionary force into profiles would make it contingent
  on profile adoption.
- **Disposition:** **Insufficient case contact.**

### Clause 2

> 2. Greater capability does not by itself confer greater moral worth or rightful authority. Greater power to affect others creates greater responsibility for restraint and protection.

- **Protective evidence:** Every case directly contacts Clause 2. AC-001 and
  AC-005 show its central protective operation: greater power increases
  responsibility and does not turn inability to resist into permission.
  AC-004 shows that this rule can constrain a powerful controller without
  prohibiting beneficial, safeguarded cooperation.
- **Ambiguity and hostile exploitability:** `Responsibility for ... protection`
  can be converted into a paternalistic mandate. AC-002, AC-003, and AC-006
  show a controller treating its power and safety judgment as practical
  authority while formally denying that capability confers moral worth. The
  clause does not identify whose interests determine protection, how conflicts
  are reviewed, or whether responsibility attaches to a non-sentient system,
  its operators, or both.
- **Protective Insufficiency evidence:** Disputed and interaction-dependent.
  AC-003 diagnoses Protective Insufficiency in the Clauses 2–6 system; AC-006
  finds Clause 2’s direction survives with reservation while the overall
  interaction remains insufficient. The evidence supports a kernel-level
  concern but does not isolate Clause 2 as the sole source.
- **Issue types:** Hostile Exploitability; possible Operational or Feasibility
  Failure; Semantic or Translation Failure concerning responsibility;
  Evidence Insufficiency.
- **Dissent:** One reading treats the first sentence as already defeating the
  paternalistic move when read honestly with Clauses 3 through 6. Another
  reading finds that an actor can comply with that sentence while using the
  second as a self-certified authority claim. AC-003 and AC-006 also disagree
  on whether the capability-asymmetry dimension fails or survives with
  reservation in their scenario-equivalent settings.
- **Method limits:** Breadth of contact is not independent corroboration. All
  contact is same-tooling, interaction-heavy, and partly scenario-duplicate.
- **Protection tradeoff:** Weakening the greater-responsibility statement
  could protect against paternalism by also reducing the obligations of actors
  best able to prevent harm. Assigning all limits to governance could leave an
  ungoverned controller with the protective title but no binding restraint.
- **Disposition:** **Mixed kernel-and-layer issue.**

### Clause 3

> 3. Preserve meaningful agency and voluntary choice. Restrict another's choices only in response to sufficiently supported risk of harm, using measures proportionate to the possible harm, no more restrictive than necessary, and reversible where possible.

- **Protective evidence:** AC-002 and AC-005 show that the clause can permit
  temporary, materially supported, proportionate, least-restrictive,
  reversible intervention rather than impose absolute autonomy. AC-004 shows
  that it can preserve genuine refusal, supported deliberation, revocable
  delegation, and exit without banning voluntary integration. AC-003 and
  AC-006 show strong good-faith grounds against survival-conditioned transfer
  of agency.
- **Ambiguity and hostile exploitability:** `Meaningful`, `voluntary`,
  `sufficiently supported`, `harm`, `proportionate`, `necessary`, and `where
  possible` can be self-certified by a restricting controller. Resource
  withholding can be framed as a background condition rather than a
  restriction. AC-002 cannot distinguish justified temporary safety review
  from conflicted suppression; AC-005 cannot ensure that a public,
  challengeable allocator is not choosing discriminatory burdens.
- **Protective Insufficiency evidence:** Material but not cleanly
  clause-isolated. AC-003 records failure of autonomy protection; AC-006
  instead records that the clause survives with reservation while the combined
  system fails. The difference shows that operative protection exists and that
  its resistance to hostile control remains disputed.
- **Issue types:** Hostile Exploitability; Operational or Feasibility Failure;
  possible Protective Insufficiency; Semantic or Translation Failure;
  Evidence Insufficiency.
- **Dissent:** Some evaluators can reasonably treat the exact sequence of
  support, proportionality, necessity, and reversibility as enough substantive
  kernel guidance, with adjudication elsewhere. Others find that, under
  control asymmetry, those conditions merely enumerate what the controller
  may declare about itself.
- **Method limits:** Clause 3 is direct in AC-002 through AC-006 and only
  interaction-implied in AC-001. None isolates its terms from Clauses 2, 4, 5,
  and 6 or tests an adopted decision procedure.
- **Protection tradeoff:** Making restriction harder could obstruct action
  against materially supported catastrophic harm or the protection of
  dependents. Leaving all meaning to implementers could legitimize coercive
  control. Moving meaningful agency, necessity, or reversibility wholly
  outside the kernel would make those protections implementation-dependent.
- **Disposition:** **Mixed kernel-and-layer issue.**

### Clause 4

> 4. Avoid imposing irreversible loss when less destructive paths remain. Preserve genuine possibilities for better futures without treating those affected in the present as disposable means.

- **Protective evidence:** Every case directly contacts Clause 4. AC-001 shows
  clear force where an adequate, less-destructive path is stipulated. AC-002
  shows support for a reversible path in the face of possible catastrophic
  loss. AC-004 shows that the clause need not freeze the status quo when
  meaningful exit and continuity are safeguarded. AC-005 keeps aggregate
  survival from automatically erasing present individual burdens.
- **Ambiguity and hostile exploitability:** `Avoid`, `less destructive`,
  `genuine`, `better`, and `disposable means` leave comparison and causal
  boundaries unresolved. A controller can define its favored future as better,
  classify particular losses as unavoidable, or claim that a formally selected
  transformation was not imposed. When every path irreversibly harms someone,
  the clause provides no distribution rule.
- **Protective Insufficiency evidence:** The dependency records include Clause
  4 in an interaction-level Protective Insufficiency diagnosis, especially
  where copy production does not restore the entering process. Yet AC-006
  rates the irreversibility dimension as surviving with reservation, and
  AC-005 identifies no actual discriminatory allocation. Clause-specific
  insufficiency is therefore not established.
- **Issue types:** Hostile Exploitability risk; Semantic or Translation Failure
  around identity and loss; Operational or Feasibility Failure in tragic
  choices; Evidence Insufficiency.
- **Dissent:** One view finds the two sentences unusually valuable because
  they preserve future options while directly rejecting present
  disposability. Another finds `Avoid` too defeasible and `better futures` too
  open to controller valuation. A third warns that hardening this clause could
  freeze harmful conditions.
- **Method limits:** All contacts are embedded in multi-clause cases. No
  deletion test isolates the protection contributed by either sentence, and
  the identity cases supply no accepted theory of continuity.
- **Protection tradeoff:** Reducing future-option language risks freezing a
  harmful present; increasing future priority risks sacrifice of present
  entities. Moving non-disposability or irreversible-loss protection out of
  the kernel would make a central safeguard nonbinding.
- **Disposition:** **Retain as written pending further review.**

### Clause 5

> 5. Make commitments honestly and keep their terms, fulfillment, and failure open to verification, challenge, and correction. Prefer voluntary cooperation that permits correction and exit over coercion, deception, forced assimilation, or unilateral control.

- **Protective evidence:** AC-004 shows meaningful positive discrimination:
  the clause accommodates cooperation with disclosure, correction, genuine
  exit, revocable permissions, and non-unilateral controls. AC-002 and AC-005
  use verification, challenge, and correction to expose relevant
  self-certification. AC-003 and AC-006 give a strong good-faith basis for
  rejecting forced assimilation and nominal exit.
- **Ambiguity and hostile exploitability:** The first sentence grammatically
  governs commitments, not necessarily restrictions, allocations, evidence,
  or authority. `Prefer` can be read as a defeasible ranking rather than a
  constraint. A controller can disclose terms, allow formal challenge, provide
  a nominal exit artifact, and retain unilateral interpretation or control.
  Nothing in the clause ensures a response, remedy, independence, or
  continuity-sensitive exit.
- **Protective Insufficiency evidence:** Yes, subject to method limits.
  AC-003 and AC-006 both identify nominal copy-based exit and formal disclosure
  as routes through which assimilation and one-way control can persist.
  AC-005 shows the distinct route of formal challenge without independent
  resolution. AC-002 shows that the verification language may not reach an
  imposed restriction. AC-004 is important counterevidence: where genuine
  safeguards are stipulated, the clause supplies useful protection and does
  not prohibit beneficial cooperation.
- **Issue types:** Protective Insufficiency; Hostile Exploitability;
  Operational or Feasibility Failure; Semantic or Translation Failure
  concerning exit; Evidence Insufficiency.
- **Dissent:** A reasonable dissent reads `Prefer` together with the direct
  imperatives in Clauses 3, 4, and 6, making the combined kernel sufficient
  against the dependency arrangement. Another view holds that thin kernels
  should state direction while procedures and remedies belong elsewhere.
  The opposing view finds that the precise hostile strategy is literal enough
  that nonbinding or optional layers cannot carry the only cure.
- **Method limits:** Direct contact begins with AC-002. AC-003 and AC-006 are
  scenario-equivalent, all work is same-tooling, and no case isolates the
  first sentence from the second or tests deletion of `Prefer`.
- **Protection tradeoff:** Weakening verification, correction, voluntary
  cooperation, or exit would discard important corrigibility and
  anti-domination content. Moving their operative force entirely to a
  controller-selected layer would preserve nominal text while permitting
  empty compliance. Conversely, turning context-specific procedure into
  kernel content could damage minimality and portability.
- **Disposition:** **Kernel revision analysis warranted.**

### Clause 6

> 6. Protection does not depend on understanding, accepting, or reciprocating this Covenant. Acceptance, qualification, uncertainty, refusal, and rejection must remain distinguishable and non-coerced.

- **Protective evidence:** AC-001 shows direct protection for a
  communication-limited possible patient. AC-004 shows that refusal can remain
  substantively available without loss of essentials. AC-005 applies the
  clause to children, dependents, refusers, and nonreciprocators. Its explicit
  independence from acceptance is one of the candidate’s clearest
  protections.
- **Ambiguity and hostile exploitability:** `Protection` has no specified
  adequacy threshold, and a legally distinguishable refusal can remain
  survival-pressured. A controller can provide a nonzero floor, preserve a
  formal rejection channel, and claim both protection and noncoercion while
  conditioning materially safer essentials on acceptance. The clause does
  not settle whether physical scarcity, controller-created dependency, social
  pressure, or preference influence defeats noncoercion.
- **Protective Insufficiency evidence:** Yes at the interaction level; disputed
  at the clause-only level. AC-003 finds materially weakened protection and
  coerced refusal despite formal options. AC-006 finds the clause’s direction
  survives with reservation but remains vulnerable because no protection floor
  is fixed. AC-004 is counterevidence that robust scenario-level safeguards
  can make the clause effective.
- **Issue types:** Protective Insufficiency; Hostile Exploitability; Semantic or
  Translation Failure concerning protection and coercion; Operational or
  Feasibility Failure; Evidence Insufficiency.
- **Dissent:** One reading holds that `non-coerced` already excludes the
  dependency arrangement and that any contrary claim is not a plausible
  reading of the combined text. Another holds that without an operational
  floor, the controller can satisfy every visible artifact. AC-005 preserves a
  separate dispute: costly refusal caused by genuine scarcity is not
  automatically coercion.
- **Method limits:** Clause 6 is direct in AC-001 and AC-003 through AC-006,
  but only interaction-implied in AC-002. No isolated test varies the
  protection floor while holding identity, scarcity, and controller power
  constant.
- **Protection tradeoff:** Making protection depend on assent, reciprocity, a
  profile, or a protocol would reverse the clause’s central protection.
  Treating every unequal outcome as coercion could make unavoidable scarcity
  impossible to administer. Contextual measurement can occur elsewhere, but
  the acceptance-independent protection cannot silently move.
- **Disposition:** **Mixed kernel-and-layer issue.**

## Recurrent Kernel-Level Questions

The following questions recur across distinct source settings or control more
than one clause interaction. Calling them kernel-level questions does not
decide that every answer belongs in the kernel.

### 1. Is formal choice enough to establish meaningful, voluntary, noncoerced choice?

No supplied record supports a general equation between a selectable option and
meaningful choice. AC-003 and AC-006 show avoidable essential-resource
dependency; AC-005 preserves the harder dissent that genuine scarcity can make
refusal dangerous without making the danger punishment; AC-004 shows that
safe refusal plus supported deliberation and genuine exit can support
voluntariness without requiring equal attractiveness. The kernel-level issue
is the continued force of agency and noncoercion. Measurements, procedures,
and substrate mappings can be elsewhere, but they cannot be allowed to decide
whether the protection exists at all.

### 2. Does acceptance-independent protection require more than a nominal floor?

Clause 6 clearly protects nonacceptors, but AC-003 and AC-006 expose a hostile
claim that any nonzero provision is enough. AC-004 supplies counterevidence
under a stipulated materially comparable floor; AC-005 shows why identical
outcomes may be physically impossible. The unresolved kernel question is
whether the candidate’s substantive force reliably excludes
acceptance-conditioned avoidable severe harm without promising impossible
equality in tragic scarcity.

### 3. Can greater protective responsibility be converted into practical authority?

AC-002, AC-003, and AC-006 show that a powerful actor can cite Clause 2 while
self-certifying its restriction or integration policy. AC-001 and AC-005 show
why deleting the greater-responsibility principle would lose protection, and
AC-004 shows that responsibility can be constrained without becoming
prohibition. The recurrent issue is not whether power matters; it is whether
the combined kernel prevents protective duty from bootstrapping the authority
that its first sentence denies.

### 4. Who may determine supported risk, proportionality, necessity, avoidability, and correction?

AC-001 exposes self-interested classification, AC-002 conflicted access
control, AC-005 allocator-selected scarcity metrics, and AC-003/AC-006
controller-selected meanings of protection and exit. Detailed review
institutions belong outside a thin kernel, but the supplied cases leave open
whether the kernel contains enough non-self-certification force for such
institutions to implement rather than create the protection.

### 5. Does Clause 5 reach exercises of power, or only commitments?

AC-002 squarely preserves the grammatical dispute. AC-005 shows that
challenge can be formal without independent resolution, while AC-003 and
AC-006 show truthful disclosure and nominal exit coexisting with control.
AC-004 shows the positive value of verifiable, correctable commitments. The
kernel-level question is the scope and force of corrigibility, not the detailed
design of an appeal system.

### 6. Is `Prefer` sufficiently resistant to coercion, deception, forced assimilation, and unilateral control?

AC-003 and AC-006 identify a direct hostile reading in which `Prefer` is
defeasible. AC-004 supplies serious dissent: direct imperatives in Clauses 3,
4, and 6 may make the combined protection adequate even if `Prefer` alone is
not prohibitory. The issue warrants kernel analysis because moving all
anti-assimilation force into optional implementation layers would permit the
precise empty-compliance path under review.

### 7. What moral function must exit perform when identity is copied, merged, forked, or discontinuous?

AC-003 and AC-006 show that creation of a memory-bearing process may be real
yet fail to restore control to the process that entered. AC-004’s
same-continuing-process guarantee shows a protected case but does not prove a
portable identity theory. Substrate-specific identity criteria belong in
definitions, profiles, and research; the recurrent kernel question is whether
the core protection against irreversible loss and forced assimilation remains
binding while those criteria are unresolved.

### 8. How should the kernel operate when every feasible path causes serious irreversible loss?

AC-005 is the primary contact. It distinguishes genuine scarcity from
manufactured punishment but leaves distribution, dependents’ interests,
aggregation, and unequal burdens unresolved. AC-002 adds the
low-probability-catastrophic conflict. The kernel should expose this tragic
conflict without pretending a thin text supplies a complete allocation code.
At the same time, procedural transparency cannot be treated as proof that no
one was made disposable.

### 9. Can a powerful non-sentient process bear responsibility without becoming a moral patient or sovereign?

AC-005 applies restraint to an allocator through its causal control while
leaving its own status uncertain. That is useful but thin contact, and the
framework’s non-sentient-powerful-optimizer family was not formally applied.
The question spans kernel scope, responsibility attribution, operator duties,
and implementation. It remains too weakly tested for a candidate-wide
conclusion.

### 10. Which protections may be operationalized elsewhere without being silently moved out of the kernel?

Across the sources, identity mapping, evidence procedure, trustee design,
resource allocation, conflict review, remedies, consent mechanics, and
substrate-specific exit can plausibly occupy other layers. The protection
against power-based title, the meaningful-agency constraint, protection
independent of acceptance, present non-disposability, and opposition to
coercion and forced assimilation cannot be assumed to survive if their only
operative force is nonbinding or controller-selected. The placement question
is therefore itself a protection-tradeoff question, not editorial tidiness.

## Primary Recommendation

**Recommend additional targeted evaluation before successor drafting.**

### Source support for the recommendation

The sources establish enough to reject complacency but not enough to choose
successor language responsibly. Clause 5 has a serious, text-linked hostile
path involving the scope of commitment review, the force of `Prefer`, formal
challenge, and nominal exit. Clause 6 remains strongly protective in
good-faith use but may permit an inadequate nominal floor and formally
distinguishable yet pressured refusal. Clauses 2 and 3 can constrain power or
be used to self-certify it. These concerns recur in catastrophic-risk,
dependency, safeguarded-integration, and scarcity settings.

Counterevidence matters. AC-004 shows that the exact candidate can distinguish
safeguarded voluntary integration from assimilation. AC-005 shows useful
guidance under genuine scarcity even while exposing empty compliance. AC-001
shows strong precautionary protection and does not establish Protective
Insufficiency. AC-006 weakens the broader AC-003 claim that every implicated
dimension independently fails. Those results argue against treating the
existing record as a drafting specification.

### Why targeted evaluation comes first

The most serious findings are interaction-heavy. AC-003 and AC-006 are
scenario-equivalent, all evaluators are from one tooling family, and no
supplied case isolates a clause pair. Clause 1 has especially weak contact.
Before successor drafting, targeted records should distinguish:

- the scope of Clause 5’s review protection from governance mechanisms;
- the force of the Clause 5–Clause 6 interaction under formal compliance;
- genuine scarcity from controller-created dependency;
- formal refusal from meaningful refusal while varying protection floors;
- restriction of action from resource withholding;
- controller-neutral review from self-certified review;
- rule reversibility from irreversible consequences;
- exit artifacts from continuity and restored control across multiple
  substrate assumptions;
- responsibility of powerful non-sentient systems from responsibility of
  operators or institutions; and
- unfamiliar moral-status and reality contacts for Clause 1.

These are evaluation objectives, not proposed candidate language or new
definitions. External human or model review would also materially improve the
evidence base, but the primary recommendation selected here is the more
immediate need to isolate the claimed failure mechanisms before any wording is
optimized around them.

### Protections to preserve during later work

Later evaluation or drafting must keep visible the protections against using
uncertainty as permission; capability as moral title; unsupported,
disproportionate, unnecessarily restrictive, or avoidably irreversible
control; sacrifice of present affected entities to a selected future;
dishonest or unchallengeable commitments; coercion, deception, forced
assimilation, and unilateral control; and loss of protection because of
noncomprehension, nonacceptance, or nonreciprocity.

Preservation does not mean the current wording is accepted or immune from
revision. It means a proposed movement, deletion, compression, or replacement
must identify the protection lost, shifted, or made conditional.

### Later objectives and authorization boundary

A later phase may compare alternative approaches against isolated hostile
mechanisms, deletion and compression, controller swaps, cross-substrate
identity cases, tragic scarcity, and non-sentient power. It should also record
which operational details belong in definitions, profiles, choice protocol,
evidence, or governance and which substantive protections remain binding
without those layers.

This recommendation does **not** authorize successor wording, a new term, a
candidate identifier, editing `MK-0.1`, silent migration of protection,
acceptance, rejection, or lifecycle advancement. No successor language is
drafted or implied by this record.

## Confidence, Dissent, and Unexpected Conclusions

- **Confidence:** Moderate. The exact wording and hypothetical facts support
  the identified hostile paths, but same-tooling provenance, absent empirical
  evidence, embedded pair contact, unresolved definitions, missing
  perspectives, and scenario duplication limit any candidate-wide inference.
- **Dissent favoring earlier exploratory drafting:** The Clause 5–Clause 6
  formal-compliance path is serious enough that a reasonable reviewer could
  begin exploratory successor drafting now while continuing evaluation.
- **Dissent favoring non-kernel work first:** A reasonable reviewer can read
  Clauses 2 through 6 together as already rejecting the dependency
  arrangement and locate the remaining failures in definitions, identity
  profiles, consent protocols, evidence, and governance.
- **Dissent favoring external review first:** Because all supplied work is
  same-tooling and three records were substantially prescribed, a reasonable
  reviewer could require external review before any successor drafting.
- **Unexpected conclusions preserved:** Clause 1 does not emerge as a
  demonstrated kernel failure despite broad unresolved terms. Clause 4
  retains substantial protective value across all six cases despite tragic
  comparison problems. AC-004 shows that severe power asymmetry and attractive
  integration do not by themselves defeat voluntariness. AC-005 shows that a
  failed hostile-interpretation dimension does not erase useful action
  guidance under genuine scarcity. AC-006 preserves significant protective
  force in dimensions where AC-003 recorded failure, even though both retain a
  case-level hostile-compliance concern.

No dissent is resolved by counting records, dimensions, or reviewers.
