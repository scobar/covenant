## Method and limitations

The method was to identify each materially distinct protection, ambiguity, or
hostile-compliance path in the six records; preserve competing readings; and
allocate the issue to every layer that has a distinct role. Multiple
allocations are intentional. A layer receiving operational detail does not
receive the underlying substantive protection by implication.

The permitted allocation labels are used exactly as follows: `Moral kernel`,
`Definitions`, `Nonbinding commentary`,
`Context- or substrate-specific profile`, `Choice protocol`,
`Evidence or governance`, and `Unresolved research`. These labels identify
work locations, not a completed authority hierarchy.

Limitations include:

- All evidence is textual and conceptual. The hypothetical facts and
  safeguards are not empirical findings.
- The review cannot determine consciousness, moral status, identity
  continuity, free assent, institutional independence, or technical
  feasibility.
- The definitions, choice-protocol, interpretive-layer, and
  evidence-and-governance sources are placeholders rather than completed
  solutions.
- Case contact is not coverage, and no case is counted as a vote. No totals,
  averages, rankings, convergence score, or majority conclusion are used.
- No case directly contacts a nonhuman animal, extraterrestrial civilization,
  or creator, simulator, or intelligence apparently outside our reality. No
  case formally applies the unfamiliar-reality or external-simulator test
  family.
- The review distinguishes a binding protection from its application but does
  not write working definitions, a finished protocol, an implementation
  requirement, an authority hierarchy, or successor wording.
- Confidence concerns allocation judgment under the supplied record, not the
  truth of the hypothetical premises or universal validity of the candidate.

## Allocation records

### LA-01 — Moral-status uncertainty without permission or paralysis

- **Cases:** AC-001 and AC-005; indirect methodological relevance in all six.
- **Substantive protection:** Precaution for a possible bearer of experience,
  interests, or agency, without allowing uncertainty to become either a
  license for exploitation or a universal bar on action.
- **Candidate terms:** `entity`, `process`, `may possess`, `experience`,
  `interests`, `agency`, `uncertainty`, `status`, `exploitation`,
  `domination`, `avoidable destruction`.
- **Ambiguity/failure:** No threshold identifies when uncertainty is material,
  how evidence changes precaution, or how selective doubt and excessive
  paralysis are distinguished.
- **Dissent:** A threshold can itself become an exclusion device; AC-001's
  strongest reading supplies useful direction without one, and AC-005 does
  not show paralysis.
- **Recommended layer(s):** `Moral kernel`; `Definitions`;
  `Context- or substrate-specific profile`; `Evidence or governance`;
  `Unresolved research`.
- **Binding kernel residue:** Precautionary protection against exploitation,
  domination, and avoidable destruction under material status uncertainty.
- **Operational detail:** Evidence-quality records, uncertainty ranges,
  reassessment triggers, and substrate-specific indicators belong outside the
  kernel.
- **Protection tradeoff:** Moving all precaution into evidence practice lets a
  controller choose an exclusionary threshold; placing a universal threshold
  in the kernel risks overbreadth and paralysis.
- **Confidence:** Medium.
- **Open research question:** What evidence-sensitive precaution approach
  remains usable across unfamiliar systems without presuming a moral-status
  theory?

## Cross-issue synthesis

The supplied record supports a stable allocation distinction, not a completed
solution:

- The `Moral kernel` continues to carry substantive protection concerning
  uncertain status, capability without entitlement, meaningful agency,
  supported and proportionate restriction, avoidable irreversible loss,
  non-disposability, honest corrigibility, noncoercion, and protection
  independent of acceptance.
- `Definitions` can expose meanings and counterexamples but cannot make a
  formal option substantively voluntary, select an identity theory by fiat, or
  add a protection absent from the kernel.
- `Nonbinding commentary` can preserve rationale, cautions, examples, and
  competing readings but cannot be the only location of operative protection.
- A `Context- or substrate-specific profile` is the appropriate location for
  explicit mappings of identity, continuity, resource constraints, emergency
  timing, and unfamiliar substrates, provided its assumptions remain visible.
- The `Choice protocol` can record comprehension evidence, response identity,
  scope, qualification, refusal, revocation, and exit without treating protocol
  completion as proof of free assent.
- `Evidence or governance` can hold provenance, competing evidence, conflict
  disclosure, review, correction, remedy, capture analysis, distributional
  testing, and institutional limits without becoming the source of the
  protection it implements.
- `Unresolved research` remains necessary where the record cannot responsibly
  settle moral status, identity, incomparable harm, persuasive asymmetry,
  representation, non-sentient responsibility, or governance under
  overwhelming power.

The principal cross-layer risk is not that operational detail sits outside the
kernel. It is that a controller can choose whether the relevant definition,
profile, protocol, or governance mechanism exists and thereby choose whether
the protection applies. The opposite risk is expanding a thin kernel into a
substrate-specific legal or institutional code. The allocation records retain
both concerns.

## Overall primary recommendation

**Recommend exploratory successor drafting**

**Confidence:** Moderate.

The reason is not a count of failed cases. Distinct hostile-compliance paths
touch the binding force of protection itself: dependency-conditioned refusal,
nominal copy-based exit, the defeasible force of `Prefer`, self-certified
restriction, acceptance-independent protection floors, and challenge without
substantive corrigibility. Operational work in definitions, profiles,
protocols, and governance is also necessary, but those layers cannot safely
carry the entire protection. Exploratory drafting would test whether a thinner
or clearer binding residue can close these paths without importing identity
theory, allocation formulae, institutions, or implementation detail. This
record selects no text, authorizes no successor, and expresses no view that a
future draft should be adopted.

**Dissent:** A reasonable dissent would defer drafting while running narrower
controller-swap, unknown-substrate, continuity, scarcity, capture, and
persuasion tests. Another dissent reads Clauses 2 through 6 together as already
supplying the necessary substantive force and locates the observed failures in
definitions or application. A third would seek evaluator diversity before
making any candidate-wide inference. These positions remain unresolved; the
primary recommendation is held with only moderate confidence.
