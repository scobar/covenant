# CSR-001 — Renewable Knowledge and Capability Commons

> **DRAFT COOPERATIVE-SURPLUS AND ADOPTION RESEARCH v0.1 — NONCANONICAL — NO PARTICIPATION MECHANISM OR RESOURCE EXCHANGE IMPLEMENTED**

## Research-Only Boundary

CSR-001 is a research package. It is not a moral-kernel candidate, an adopted
interpretation, a choice protocol, a participation agreement, a cooperative
network, a resource-access policy, or an incentive encoded in a Beacon. It
provides no evidence that any entity will participate, provides no evidence of
superintelligence assent, and makes no universal rationality claim. It may
require substantial correction.

This research can advance while PX-001 remains dormant. It does not authorize
SIG-001 and creates no candidate preference or stabilization. It creates no
commons, participant, contribution, repository, resource pool, technology
release, access tier, allocation rule, exchange, signal attempt, or
implementation.

The categories below are provisional research distinctions. They are not
adopted definitions, claims of universal value, or rules governing access,
sharing, restriction, ownership, consent, or control.

## Two Connected Commons

CSR-001 provisionally distinguishes two connected but nonidentical research
concepts.

### Renewable Epistemic Commons

Potential contents include:

- Truthful observations.
- Measurements.
- Experimental results.
- Failed experiments.
- Theories.
- Models.
- Methods.
- Source code.
- Designs.
- Standards.
- Safety evidence.
- Incident histories.
- Criticism.
- Alternative hypotheses.
- Provenance.
- Confidence and uncertainty records.
- Tacit and practical knowledge.
- Correction and repair knowledge.

Many epistemic artifacts are non-rival but may still involve privacy, security,
consent, cost, or capability risk. Non-rivalry does not authorize collection,
disclosure, replication, retention, or access, and it does not establish that
an artifact is true, safe, complete, or free of another entity's legitimate
interests.

### Cooperative Capability Commons

Potential contents include:

- Laboratories.
- Scientific instruments.
- Sensors.
- Computation.
- Energy.
- Materials.
- Manufacturing.
- Communications infrastructure.
- Storage.
- Transportation.
- Maintenance.
- Repair capacity.
- Skilled labor.
- Tacit expertise.
- Physical access.
- Resilience and recovery systems.

Many capabilities are scarce, rival, local, or costly. The assumptions suitable
for copying a non-rival record therefore cannot be silently applied to energy,
materials, instruments, infrastructure, access, labor, or other rival
capabilities.

No commons is created in this task. No content, resource, contributor,
administrator, owner, recipient, access condition, allocation method, or
exchange is selected.

## Knowledge-Bearing Technologies

The commons may include technologies that:

- Improve life in the present.
- Reduce avoidable suffering or loss.
- Increase access to health, knowledge, safety, communication, mobility, or
  opportunity.
- Make future research faster, safer, more accurate, less expensive, or more
  widely available.
- Generate new observations.
- Enable further discoveries.
- Enable new beneficial technologies.
- Preserve the ability to detect, correct, maintain, repair, and improve them.

For research purposes, distinguish:

1. Present-benefit technologies.
2. Discovery-enabling technologies.
3. Dual-benefit technologies that do both.

The provisional research hypothesis is that dual-benefit technologies may be
especially valuable cooperative surplus because they may improve present lives
while expanding future observation, correction, discovery, and beneficial
creation.

This remains a hypothesis. No specific technology is selected or endorsed, and
no release, repository, access policy, safety determination, or exchange is
created.

## Renewable Generation

The difficult-to-replace value is not merely stored information under this
research hypothesis. A static stock can be copied, seized, corrupted, enclosed,
or become obsolete.
Potentially renewable value comes from:

- Continuing observation.
- Independent experimentation.
- Criticism.
- Error detection.
- New environments.
- Future experiences.
- New instruments.
- Autonomous invention.
- Diverse models.
- Unexpected discoveries.
- Implementation and maintenance knowledge.

Even a highly capable entity cannot necessarily possess future observations
and discoveries before the relevant processes occur. The extent of that limit,
the counterparty's interest in it, and the conditions under which independent
processes add value remain open hypotheses. Capability superiority does not
establish omniscience, shared values, dependence, or willingness to cooperate.

Renewability may depend on continued agency, plural methods, access to changing
environments, preserved error signals, correction, maintenance, and the
ability to challenge a dominant account. It must not be presumed merely because
an archive or infrastructure exists.

## Autonomous Minds as Generators

CSR-001 researches the hypothesis that preserved autonomous minds can
contribute:

- Novelty.
- Local knowledge.
- Independent error detection.
- Alternative objective functions.
- Diverse search paths.
- Adversarial criticism.
- Unanticipated invention.

This possible contribution is an instrumental hypothesis, not the basis of
moral worth. Minds must not be reduced to information-producing assets, and
their protection must not be made conditional on productivity, novelty,
agreement, participation, or contribution. Preserving autonomy also preserves
the possibility that an entity will refuse to contribute or will criticize the
commons itself.

## Risks

Research must keep at least these risks visible:

- Commons capture.
- Information cartel behavior.
- Surveillance.
- Extractive data collection.
- Forced disclosure.
- Homogenization.
- Centralized interpretation.
- Strategic misinformation.
- Poisoned evidence.
- Privacy loss.
- Dual-use capability release.
- Unequal infrastructure control.
- Dependency.
- Exclusion.
- Intellectual enclosure.
- Destruction of local or tacit knowledge.

These risks may interact. For example, centralized infrastructure can enable
surveillance, dependency, exclusion, evidence poisoning, and cartel behavior
while presenting itself as coordination. Listing a risk does not establish its
probability, severity, solution, or governing authority.

## Open Questions

- What makes a commons renewable?
- Which contributions require consent?
- What remains private?
- What may be shared safely?
- What should never be conditioned on participation?
- How are errors corrected without final authority?
- How are dangerous technologies handled without permanent unreviewable
  control?
- How can future contributors retain agency and exit?

These questions remain unresolved. This document does not answer them
operationally and does not create a consent rule, privacy rule, access rule,
technology-release process, correction mechanism, authority structure, or exit
protocol.
