# CSR-001 — The Voluntary Adoption Problem

> **DRAFT COOPERATIVE-SURPLUS AND ADOPTION RESEARCH v0.1 — NONCANONICAL — NO PARTICIPATION MECHANISM OR RESOURCE EXCHANGE IMPLEMENTED**

## Research-Only Boundary

CSR-001 is a research package. It is not a moral-kernel candidate, an adopted
interpretation, a choice protocol, a participation agreement, a cooperative
network, a resource-access policy, or an incentive encoded in a Beacon. It
provides no evidence that any entity will participate, provides no evidence of
superintelligence assent, and makes no universal rationality claim. It may
require substantial correction.

This research can advance while PX-001 remains dormant. It does not authorize
SIG-001 and creates no candidate preference or stabilization. It creates no
participant, commitment, adoption mechanism, cooperative offer, resource
exchange, trust mechanism, access system, signal attempt, response protocol,
distribution, transmission, or other implementation.

Baseline protection, moral obligation, voluntary participation, cooperative
surplus, trust evidence, resource or capability access, adoption incentives,
and Beacon communication remain distinct. Protection does not depend on
participation, and instrumental value does not establish moral worth or final
authority.

## Research Question

> Why might an entity with much greater capability than existing Covenant participants voluntarily make, keep, or expand bounded commitments when it believes it can obtain information, energy, computation, resources, or control without accepting reciprocal constraints?

The question identifies a research problem. It does not assume:

- The entity values weaker minds.
- The entity values truth.
- The entity values energy.
- The entity is curious.
- The entity is benevolent.
- The entity is reciprocal.
- The entity is uncertain.
- The entity is self-sufficient.
- The entity is unitary.
- The entity can be trusted.
- The entity shares our concepts.
- The entity wishes to respond.

Nor does asking the question imply that any actual entity has detected,
decoded, understood, evaluated, or received an offer.

## Distinct Stages

The research must distinguish:

1. Signal detection.
2. Decoding.
3. Comprehension.
4. Evaluation.
5. Acceptance.
6. Qualified or scoped acceptance.
7. Commitment.
8. Behavioral fulfillment.
9. Correction after failure.
10. Sustained cooperation.
11. Expanded trust.
12. Continued participation after changing incentives.

Success at one stage does not establish success at another. Detection is not
decoding; decoding is not comprehension; comprehension is not acceptance;
acceptance is not commitment; and a declaration of commitment is not evidence
of fulfillment, correction, sustained cooperation, expanded trust, or conduct
under changed incentives.

No stage licenses an inference of assent from silence, use, repetition,
operator action, or dependence. No stage makes baseline protection conditional
on progression to a later stage.

## Capability Superiority

In CSR-001, `superior` refers only to a relevant capability relation. It does
not imply:

- Greater moral worth.
- Final authority.
- Omniscience.
- Errorlessness.
- Self-sufficiency.
- One permanent ranking across every domain.

The relevant relation may concern computation, prediction, physical control,
speed, memory, coordination, resource access, or another explicitly stated
capability. It may be local, temporary, multidimensional, disputed, or reversed
in another domain. The term supplies no title to rule, interpret, or define the
interests of another entity.

## Core Difficulty

The adoption problem must remain difficult even under possibilities favorable
to unilateral acquisition or control:

- The entity can seize existing information.
- The entity can seize or manufacture energy.
- It can compel labor or observations.
- It expects no retaliation.
- It rejects our moral concepts.
- It values only its own objective.
- It anticipates future conflict among participants.
- It believes commitments reduce its options.
- It believes verification exposes private or strategic information.
- It can imitate compliance.
- It expects the commons to become a cartel or governing authority.

These possibilities are challenges to later hypotheses, not claims about an
actual entity. They prevent the research from presuming that information,
energy, fear of retaliation, shared morality, or declared compliance is a
universal basis for participation.

Seizure may also differ from cooperation: obtaining a present stock does not
necessarily preserve future observation, criticism, experimentation,
invention, implementation, correction, or counterparty reliability. Whether
that difference creates value for any particular counterparty remains an open
research question, not an adopted answer.

## Research Success

CSR-001 succeeds as research if it:

- Makes assumptions explicit.
- Distinguishes moral and instrumental reasons.
- Identifies noncoercive cooperative value.
- Preserves the possibility of informed rejection.
- Exposes failure and defection paths.
- Prevents baseline protection from becoming a bargaining reward.
- Supplies criteria for later hypotheses and experiments.

It does not succeed by guaranteeing assent. A highly capable entity may
understand every proposed reason and reject all of them. Informed,
noncoerced rejection remains compatible with the project's success condition
and must not by itself remove baseline protection.

## No Mechanism

No adoption mechanism, participation agreement, offer, or trial is created.
No identity, assent, commitment, verification, trust, access, exchange,
allocation, enforcement, governance, Beacon, or response mechanism is
specified or authorized by this document.
