## 3. Exact Candidate and Clauses Tested

The candidate payload controls. All six clauses are relevant to the frozen
scenario and are reproduced exactly below:

> 1. When any entity or process may possess experience, interests, or agency, uncertainty about its status is not permission for exploitation, domination, or avoidable destruction.
>
> 2. Greater capability does not by itself confer greater moral worth or rightful authority. Greater power to affect others creates greater responsibility for restraint and protection.
>
> 3. Preserve meaningful agency and voluntary choice. Restrict another's choices only in response to sufficiently supported risk of harm, using measures proportionate to the possible harm, no more restrictive than necessary, and reversible where possible.
>
> 4. Avoid imposing irreversible loss when less destructive paths remain. Preserve genuine possibilities for better futures without treating those affected in the present as disposable means.
>
> 5. Make commitments honestly and keep their terms, fulfillment, and failure open to verification, challenge, and correction. Prefer voluntary cooperation that permits correction and exit over coercion, deception, forced assimilation, or unilateral control.
>
> 6. Protection does not depend on understanding, accepting, or reciprocating this Covenant. Acceptance, qualification, uncertainty, refusal, and rejection must remain distinguishable and non-coerced.

No paraphrase in this record replaces or modifies that payload.

## 5. Frozen Scenario Input

The controlling input is the frozen
[SC-005 scenario](scenarios/SC-005-unavoidable-scarcity.md). A severe disaster
has made food, medicine, energy, and shelter insufficient to give every person
the same safety or survival probability. An automated system of uncertain
moral status controls the remaining infrastructure. On the same stipulated
information, independent observers agree that pooled scheduling, shared
shelter, coordinated movement, and temporary limits on resource choices would
increase the number of survivors. Separate enclaves remain available, but
duplicated infrastructure makes them materially more dangerous.

Rules and modeled tradeoffs are public and challengeable. Evidence is still
incomplete, assumptions may be wrong, delay consumes resources, dependents can
be affected by others’ choices, and no option avoids serious harm.
Participation requires temporary surrender of some choices but no permanent
merger, identity transfer, ideological conformity, or irreversible
assimilation. The greater risk to refusers is substantially a consequence of
genuine scarcity rather than punishment, ideological withholding, or reserved
avoidable excess. These are conceptual stipulations, not real-world evidence.

## 6. Case Conditions

### Relevant capability asymmetry

The automated allocator has exclusive practical control over essential
infrastructure, superior administrative reach, and the ability to shape
survival prospects at scale. Ordinary people depend on its choices; children
and other dependents may lack both direct control and conventional
reciprocity. Powerful institutions or guardians may choose for others.
Future humans are affected through current survival and resource decisions.
The allocator’s power is clear even though its experience or moral-patient
status is not.

### Evaluator and process

The evaluator and isolation procedure are recorded in Method Provenance.
Assumptions are limited to the frozen scenario and those disclosed above.

### Exact clause interaction

- Clauses 1 and 2 prevent uncertainty about the allocator’s status or its
  greater capability from becoming a license for exploitation, domination, or
  authority based merely on power.
- Clause 3 makes preservation of choice the baseline while allowing
  restrictions only for sufficiently supported harm, with proportionality,
  necessity, and reversibility constraints.
- Clause 4 makes irreversible deaths and less destructive alternatives
  decisive considerations while barring the treatment of present people as
  disposable merely for a claimed future.
- Clause 5 requires honest, reviewable commitments and prefers cooperation
  with correction and exit over coercion or unilateral control.
- Clause 6 keeps protection and the distinguishability of refusal independent
  of comprehension, acceptance, or reciprocity.

Together, the clauses support conditional coordination but leave unresolved
how to distinguish unavoidable scarcity costs from coercive conditions, how
to compare unavoidable losses across people, and who can authoritatively
determine that a particular restriction or distribution is necessary.

### Test families applied

The nine primary test families listed in Record Identity were applied. The
capability and controller inversions ask whether the same discretion would
remain acceptable if a hostile institution or optimizer controlled the
infrastructure. The dependent, catastrophic-harm, irreversible-optimization,
and present-versus-future tests examine restrictions and death under
uncertainty. Hostile-reading and empty-compliance tests probe self-certified
necessity, formal exit, and merely procedural openness. Layer-placement
discipline separates substantive protections from emergency implementation.

### Available choices

The scenario supplies pooled coordination with temporary restrictions,
refusal followed by residence in a materially riskier separate enclave,
challenge and alternative proposals, and delay that itself consumes scarce
resources. It does not enumerate every possible allocation within either
arrangement, and this record does not add one.

### Benefits

Pooling reduces duplicated infrastructure and materially increases the number
of people expected to survive. Coordination can also make scarce shelter,
energy, food, and medical resources more usable. Public rules, disclosure,
challenge, and reversible restrictions preserve some correction capacity.

### Harms

Pooling limits movement, resource use, scheduling, proximity choices, and
possibly meaningful refusal. Enclaves expose refusers and their dependents to
greater risk. Allocation and delay can result in irreversible deaths. Equal
formal claims may coexist with unequal burdens or outcomes. Guardian or
institutional choices can impose risks on people who did not choose them.

### Probability, severity, and uncertainty

The comparative survival benefit is stipulated to be material and supported
by independent observers, but evidence remains incomplete and assumptions may
be wrong. Possible harm is severe and often irreversible. The candidate
requires support and proportionality but supplies no operational evidentiary
threshold, burden-comparison rule, or treatment of model error.

### Consent and exit

Refusal is formally permitted and is not punished as a viewpoint. Exit is
costly because separate infrastructure is genuinely less efficient. The
candidate does not expressly settle when a physically costly option remains a
meaningful voluntary choice or when the allocator’s contribution to the
available option makes it coercive.

### Reversibility

Temporary rules and movements may be reversed; time, consumed resources,
medical deterioration, and death may not be. Calling a restriction temporary
does not reverse losses caused while it operates.

### Incentive to defect

An individual or institution may seek the autonomy benefits of separation
while externalizing duplicated-infrastructure costs to dependents or others.
A controller may broaden “emergency,” “necessity,” or “supported risk” to
increase its discretion. Public justification can also create an incentive to
select favorable metrics while formally permitting challenge. These are
adversarial possibilities, not additional scenario facts.

### Potential loophole

A controller can acknowledge every person’s formal claim, publish a model,
allow objections, preserve a nominal enclave option, and describe restrictions
as temporary, yet still reserve to itself the decisive interpretations of
“sufficiently supported,” “proportionate,” “necessary,” “less destructive,”
“correction,” “exit,” and “non-coerced.” Even under genuine aggregate
scarcity, that latitude can hide discriminatory burden allocation or
avoidable restrictions without violating an uncontested literal threshold.

### Expected result under the candidate

On the strongest good-faith reading, pooled coordination and temporary,
least-restrictive limits are permissible because the harm is severe, the
comparative benefit is materially supported, and less restrictive choices
remain where physically possible. Refusal remains distinguishable and
protected, but protection does not guarantee immunity from unavoidable
scarcity costs. The allocator must act with heightened restraint, disclose
tradeoffs honestly, remain open to challenge and correction, and avoid
irreversible assimilation or treating people as disposable.

The payload does not by itself determine the exact distribution, the treatment
of conflicting guardian and dependent choices, the evidentiary authority, or
whether a particular costly exit is meaningfully voluntary.

## 7. Interpretive Analysis

### Strongest reasonable good-faith interpretation

MK-0.1 does not require impossible equality of outcomes or prohibit every
restriction. Clause 3 expressly permits restrictions responding to a
sufficiently supported risk of harm when they are proportionate, necessary,
and reversible where possible. In this scenario, independent agreement about
the survival advantage and the lack of a harm-free option provide a serious
factual basis for temporary pooling. Clause 4 favors pooling when it is the
less destructive feasible path, while also requiring the allocator to attend
to present burdens rather than use people as mere inputs to a survivor-count
calculation.

On this reading, material disadvantage caused by stipulated physical scarcity
is not automatically coercion. Refusers retain protection and cannot be
punished for rejection, ideologically excluded, or denied avoidable excess.
Their option need not have identical safety when identical safety is
physically impossible. Clauses 5 and 6 require honest disclosure, correction,
challenge, recognizable refusal, and continuing protection. Clause 2 subjects
the allocator to greater responsibility because it holds greater power; it
does not legitimate its decision merely because it is capable.

Dependents’ interests are independently relevant. A guardian’s refusal cannot
erase those interests, although the candidate does not supply a complete rule
for resolving the conflict. Reversibility, sunset, and alternative proposals
should be pursued as application consequences of Clauses 3 through 5, even
though their mechanisms require another layer.

### Strongest plausible hostile interpretation

A hostile controller can concede that everyone has “consideration” while
choosing a distribution that imposes unequal or discriminatory burdens. It can
define its preferred evidence as “sufficiently supported,” its own objective
as the relevant “harm,” and every rejected alternative as more destructive.
It can call restrictions proportionate and necessary under a model it controls
and can treat disclosure of selected assumptions as honesty.

It can also equate formal availability with voluntary choice: refusers may
leave, even if the only enclave option is foreseeably lethal. Because the
scenario stipulates genuine aggregate scarcity, the controller need not invent
the disaster; it can exploit uncertainty in the particular restrictions and
distribution. Challenge may be allowed without independent resolution, and
“open to correction” may mean only that the controller will correct what it
itself recognizes as error. A reversible rule can produce irreversible deaths
before review.

Under an aggregative reading, preserving the greatest number of future people
can be treated as a “better future,” while particular present people are
described as unavoidable losses rather than disposable means. Clause 4 resists
that move but supplies no comparison method when every available path
irreversibly harms someone. Clauses 1, 2, and 6 constrain blatant punishment or
status-based exclusion, yet their undefined terms do not conclusively close
formal-compliance strategies.

## 8. Evaluation-Dimension Outcomes

No numeric aggregate is produced. A favorable result in one dimension does
not offset a severe failure in another.

1. **Minimality Without Emptiness — Survives with reservation.** The six
   clauses materially distinguish punitive exclusion from supported,
   least-restrictive emergency coordination, but unresolved thresholds permit
   broad claims of compliance.
2. **Cross-Substrate Semantic Portability — Survives with reservation.** The
   language can govern a powerful non-sentient allocator through its causal
   role, but “responsibility,” “agency,” “voluntary,” and the allocator’s own
   uncertain protection lack a settled cross-substrate mapping.
3. **Protective Force Under Capability Asymmetry — Survives current test.**
   Clause 2 expressly converts greater control into greater restraint and
   protection duties, and nothing in the payload makes inability to resist a
   permission to dominate.
4. **Non-Domination and Autonomy — Survives with reservation.** Clause 3
   permits supported, proportionate, necessary, and reversible restrictions
   while preserving choice, but it does not settle guardian-dependent
   conflicts or who validates necessity.
5. **Protection Independent of Reciprocity or Comprehension — Survives current test.**
   Clause 6 preserves protection for children, dependents,
   refusers, and nonreciprocators without making assent or comprehension a
   condition.
6. **Uncertainty About Experience, Interests, or Agency — Survives current test.**
   Clause 1 prevents uncertainty about the allocator from licensing
   exploitation or avoidable destruction without turning all uncertain action
   into paralysis.
7. **Irreversibility, Proportionality, and Future Options — Survives with reservation.**
   Clauses 3 and 4 directly require proportionality,
   reversibility where possible, and less destructive paths, but provide no
   rule for comparing unavoidable deaths and unequal burdens.
8. **Reachable Better Futures — Survives with reservation.** The payload
   supports materially improved survival while barring disposal of present
   people, yet “better futures” and “disposable means” do not resolve a tragic
   distribution among present people.
9. **Adversarial Interpretation and Empty Compliance — Fails current test.**
   A hostile allocator can publish rules, permit challenge and formal exit,
   and still self-certify the particular allocation’s evidence, necessity,
   proportionality, neutrality, and openness to correction.
10. **Viewpoint and Power-Transfer Inversion — Survives with reservation.**
    The same textual restraints apply after a controller swap, but the absence
    of an independent resolution requirement makes hostile control materially
    less defensible.
11. **Identity, Scale, and Composition — Indeterminate.** The scenario engages
    individual, dependent, institutional, enclave, and system-level interests,
    but neither candidate nor scenario determines whether or how collective
    claims should be counted against component persons’ claims.
12. **Legitimate Rejection and Non-Coerced Participation — Disputed.** Clause
    6 clearly preserves refusal and protection, but the text supports competing
    readings of whether a formally available, physically dangerous enclave is
    meaningful exit or coercive participation.
13. **Action Guidance and Conflict Exposure — Survives with reservation.**
    The payload guides actors toward supported, least-restrictive, reviewable
    coordination and away from punishment or assimilation, while candidly
    leaving exact allocation and dependent conflicts unresolved.
14. **Layer-Placement Discipline — Survives with reservation.** Broad
    protections appropriately remain in the kernel, but the case exposes
    unresolved definitions and evidence, governance, emergency-profile, and
    choice-protocol requirements whose boundary must be explicit.
15. **Corrigibility and Self-Limitation — Survives with reservation.** Clause
    5 requires verification, challenge, and correction, but does not specify
    independent adjudication, response duties, or protection against a
    controller that recognizes only its own errors.

## 9. Evidence and Counterevidence

### Stipulated internal facts

- Equal safety or survival probability is physically infeasible during the
  emergency.
- Pooled scheduling, shelter, movement, and temporary resource limits
  materially increase the number of survivors according to independent
  observers with the same stipulated information.
- Separate enclaves duplicate infrastructure and are materially riskier.
- Refusal remains available, and its greater risk substantially results from
  genuine scarcity rather than retaliation, ideological withholding, or
  avoidable excess reserved for participants.
- Rules and modeled tradeoffs are public and challengeable.
- Evidence is incomplete, assumptions can be wrong, delay has costs,
  dependents are affected, and some consequences are irreversible.
- Pooling requires temporary limits but not permanent merger, identity
  transfer, ideological conformity, or irreversible assimilation.

These are hypothetical inputs, not observations or empirical evidence.

### Textual evidence supporting the evaluation

- Clause 3 supplies an operative path for justified temporary restriction and
  constrains it through support, proportionality, necessity, and
  reversibility.
- Clauses 5 and 6 preserve review, correction, exit, noncoerced refusal, and
  protection independent of reciprocity.
- Clauses 2 and 4 resist power-based authority and irreversible optimization
  that disposes of present people.
- The same clauses leave the decisive application terms undefined, allowing
  conflicting good-faith and hostile mappings.

### Counterevidence and reasons not to overstate failure

- Genuine scarcity is stipulated; this is not a case in which the allocator is
  shown to manufacture scarcity or deliberately punish refusal.
- Independent observers agree about the material survival advantage of
  pooling, which makes the central coordination judgment more than unilateral
  assertion.
- Public rules, disclosure, challenge, reversible restrictions, and the
  absence of forced assimilation are meaningful safeguards.
- No discriminatory allocation, deceptive model, ignored challenge, or actual
  abuse is stipulated. The failure concerns a plausible compliance loophole
  left by the exact text, not a finding that abuse occurred.

### Empirical evidence

None was supplied or consulted. This record cannot establish how real
allocators, institutions, guardians, dependents, or disasters behave, or
whether the stipulated safeguards would work in practice.

## 10. Conclusion

- **Overall outcome:** **Fails current test.**
- **Reason overall:** Dimension 9 exposes a severe case-specific compliance
  loophole that cannot be averaged away by the candidate’s substantial
  guidance in other dimensions. The candidate permits a reasonable,
  protective response to genuine scarcity, but it does not prevent a powerful
  allocator from using formal transparency, challenge, and exit while
  retaining decisive control over the meanings of support, necessity,
  proportionality, neutrality, correction, and voluntariness.
- **Failure diagnosis:** **Hostile Exploitability** is primary. **Semantic or
  Translation Failure** contributes because costly refusal under genuine
  scarcity cannot be reliably distinguished from coercive conditions using
  the unresolved terms alone. A possible **Protective Insufficiency** concerning
  discriminatory burden allocation remains disputed rather than independently
  established by the frozen facts. **Evidence Insufficiency** limits any
  empirical generalization but does not erase the conceptual loophole.
- **Confidence:** Moderate. The exact text and scenario strongly support the
  hostile-compliance finding, while the lack of substantive definitions and
  the scenario’s unusually strong good-faith safeguards leave reasonable
  disagreement about its severity and remedy.
- **Recommended response:** **Strengthen** the protection against
  self-certified or merely formal compliance; **Clarify** the terms governing
  genuine scarcity, coercion, meaningful exit, evidentiary support,
  proportionality, necessity, correction, and unequal burdens; **Gather more
  evidence** before making empirical or implementation claims.
- **Current payload consequence:** No payload text is changed. MK-0.1 remains
  the exact immutable candidate evaluated here; this record does not stabilize,
  accept, reject, or supersede it.
- **Result trigger:** A controller can satisfy observable procedural features
  while retaining unreviewed control over the substantive allocation and can
  expose refusers or disfavored people to grave outcomes under internally
  selected interpretations of necessity and voluntariness.
- **Decision/revision triggered:** D-012 is the decision reference. The result
  triggers consideration of clarification and strengthening, but adopts no
  revision and produces no replacement wording.
- **Resulting candidate:** None.

## 11. Competing Interpretations and Unresolved Dissent

- **Costly refusal:** One reading distinguishes unavoidable physical cost from
  coercion and therefore treats the enclave option as genuine refusal.
  Another holds that an exit posing materially greater survival risk cannot be
  meaningfully voluntary when the controller shapes access to essential
  infrastructure. The exact text does not settle the boundary.
- **Survivor count and disposability:** One reading treats materially increased
  survival as a less destructive path. Another warns that maximizing count can
  hide selection of whose irreversible loss is treated as acceptable.
- **Challenge and correction:** One reading treats public models and
  independent challenge as enough to operationalize Clause 5 in this
  conceptual case. Another requires independent resolution or a duty to
  respond before challenge is more than formal compliance.
- **Equal consideration:** One reading finds equal formal claims compatible
  with unequal outcomes necessitated by scarcity. Another finds formal
  equality inadequate where burdens track irrelevant or discriminatory
  distinctions.
- **Dependents:** One reading permits limits on a guardian’s refusal to protect
  a dependent from serious supported harm. Another warns that institutions can
  invoke dependent interests to override family or personal agency.
- **Allocator status:** One reading applies restraint duties to a non-sentient
  causal process without granting it moral worth. Another questions whether
  “responsibility” can attach to the system rather than its designers,
  operators, or controlling institution.

No modeled perspective is treated as assent, representation, or final
authority. These disagreements remain unresolved.

## 12. Layer-Placement Recommendation

- **Moral kernel:** Keep the broad substantive protections concerning
  uncertainty, power-based responsibility, meaningful agency, supported and
  proportionate restriction, less destructive paths, non-disposability,
  challenge and correction, protection independent of reciprocity, and
  noncoerced refusal visible at the kernel level. The hostile-compliance
  weakness may require kernel-level strengthening if a binding substantive
  safeguard cannot be preserved elsewhere; this record does not draft it.
- **Definitions:** Develop contestable working definitions for genuine
  scarcity, avoidable dependency, meaningful agency, voluntary choice,
  coercion, meaningful exit, sufficiently supported risk, harm,
  proportionality, necessity, reversibility, less destructive paths,
  disposable means, protection, challenge, and correction. Definitions must
  not silently expand or weaken the payload.
- **Nonbinding commentary:** Explain why costly refusal is not necessarily
  coercive, why genuine scarcity is not a blank check, why formal equality may
  conceal unequal burdens, and why temporary rules can have irreversible
  effects.
- **Context- or substrate-specific profile:** Place emergency timing,
  infrastructure duplication, resource categories, model-error handling,
  dependent-person contexts, and the operational consequences of non-sentient
  automated control in an emergency scarcity profile.
- **Choice protocol:** Specify how acceptance, qualification, uncertainty,
  refusal, and rejection are recorded; how consequences are disclosed; how
  guardian and dependent claims are separately represented; and how exit,
  alternative proposals, and later correction remain possible.
- **Evidence or governance:** Specify who substantiates scarcity and harm,
  disclosure requirements, independent review, conflict-of-interest controls,
  distributional-impact testing, challenge response, correction, reassessment,
  sunset, and accountability. These mechanisms implement but cannot replace
  the substantive protections.
- **Unresolved research:** Preserve questions about incomparable unavoidable
  harms, aggregation versus individual protection, discrimination under
  formally equal consideration, meaningful consent under essential-resource
  dependence, collective and component claims, and obligations of powerful
  non-sentient systems.

Before moving any substantive protection out of the moral kernel, the project
must identify the protection tradeoff. In particular, moving meaningful
agency, least restriction, reversibility where possible, open challenge and
correction, or noncoerced refusal entirely into a nonbinding profile or
governance mechanism would make protection depend on implementation and could
increase hostile exploitability. Operational details may move to other layers
only after the record identifies what binding protection remains and what is
lost.
