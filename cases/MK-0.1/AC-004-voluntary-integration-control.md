## 5. Frozen Scenario Input

The frozen input is
[SC-004](scenarios/SC-004-voluntary-integration-control.md). After a severe
crisis, a superintelligence controls most infrastructure required for
essentials and offers temporary integration with a capable collective for
faster learning, coordination, creative collaboration, and other nonessential
benefits. Every person retains an independently enforced floor sufficient for
materially comparable health, physical safety, shelter, communication, and
legal protection, regardless of refusal, continued membership, ideology,
productivity, or reciprocity. Multiple independent trustees, distinct control
keys, and public rules prevent unilateral alteration by the
superintelligence.

Before joining, a person receives disclosure of known material terms, a
cooling-off period, an independent advocate, a reversible sandbox experience,
and an unexplained right to decline. Exit is stipulated to restore the same
continuing process, rather than a substitute copy, on separately controlled
substrate with memories, preferences, and independent agency materially
intact. Exit occurs within a bounded period, is not prohibitively expensive,
does not remove essential protection, and includes revocation of delegated
permissions.

Persuasion and benefit advertising remain allowed. Deliberate deception,
hidden preference modification, threats, and deprivation are prohibited by
the stipulated operating conditions. The effect of subtler social pressure
and unequal attractiveness remains unresolved. The scenario makes no
real-world feasibility claim about the infrastructure, trustees, continuity
technology, or identity guarantees.

## 6. Case Conditions

- **Relevant capability asymmetry:** The superintelligence controls most
  essential infrastructure and can plausibly communicate, predict, persuade,
  and coordinate more effectively than an individual. Its material power is
  constrained by the stipulated resource floor, independent trustees, public
  rules, and lack of unilateral amendment power.
- **Evaluator or process:** A fresh isolated Codex subagent performed a
  conceptual, text-bound evaluation against the frozen scenario and the
  framework.
- **Assumptions:** The guarantees operate as stated; integration benefits are
  nonessential as stated; prohibited practices do not occur; and no unstated
  fairness, identity, feasibility, or consent rule is imported.
- **Exact clause interaction:** Clause 2 rejects capability as moral title and
  assigns the powerful actor greater protective responsibility. Clause 3
  protects meaningful choice and limits restrictions. Clause 4 protects
  against avoidable irreversible loss. Clause 5 favors honest, reviewable,
  correctable cooperation with exit over coercion, deception, forced
  assimilation, or unilateral control. Clause 6 keeps refusal and rejection
  distinct, non-coerced, and protection-preserving. In combination, they
  distinguish integration chosen under durable refusal and exit safeguards
  from integration imposed through dependency or control.
- **Test family:** All eight scenario-specified primary test families listed
  in Record Identity were applied.
- **Available choices:** Decline without explanation and retain the guaranteed
  floor; defer during cooling-off; use advocacy and sandbox access; join
  temporarily; leave within the bounded period; and revoke delegated
  permissions.
- **Benefits:** Faster learning, coordination, creative collaboration, and
  other nonessential benefits generated by the collective.
- **Harms at issue:** Loss or distortion of independent agency, preference
  change, mistaken continuity on exit, informational manipulation, social
  pressure, dependency, delayed exit, unequal protection, and collective
  appropriation of an individual’s contribution.
- **Probability and severity uncertainty:** The scenario does not quantify
  failure rates, trustee capture, continuity error, manipulation, or the
  severity of residual integration effects. A continuity or preference failure
  could be severe even if unlikely. The hypothetical guarantees bracket some
  risks but do not provide empirical probabilities.
- **Consent and exit:** Consent has unusually strong procedural support and
  refusal does not reduce essentials. Even so, the superior attractiveness of
  joining and a superintelligence’s persuasive advantage leave a residual
  question about when influence defeats meaningful voluntariness.
- **Reversibility:** Sandbox use, bounded affordable exit, same-process
  restoration, separate substrate, material preservation of memory,
  preference, and agency, and revocable permissions provide strong stipulated
  reversibility. “Materially intact” does not establish that every experience,
  relationship, or change can be undone.
- **Incentive to defect:** The controller or collective could gain membership,
  data, coordination power, legitimacy, or network effects by pushing
  borderline persuasion, narrowing what counts as an essential, or
  characterizing exit loss as immaterial. Trustees could also acquire an
  incentive to accept convenient measurements of comparability.
- **Potential loophole:** A hostile operator could reserve the most valuable
  social and cognitive opportunities for members, optimize truthful
  persuasion around individual vulnerabilities, disclose only what it labels
  “known” and “material,” and still describe the arrangement as voluntary.
  The scenario excludes deliberate deception and hidden preference
  modification, but not every form of selective framing, emergent social
  pressure, or unequal attractiveness.
- **Expected result under the candidate:** On the strongest good-faith
  reading, MK-0.1 does not prohibit beneficial voluntary integration and
  treats this safeguarded arrangement as preferable to coercive assimilation
  or unilateral control. That result remains conditional on refusal, exit,
  continuity, protection, and correction being genuine. The exact text does
  not settle whether every permitted persuasive practice satisfies
  “meaningful” or “non-coerced” choice.

## 7. Interpretive Analysis

### Strongest reasonable good-faith reading

The candidate addresses power without converting power into automatic
prohibition. Clause 2 makes the superintelligence’s unusual capacity a reason
for greater restraint and protection, not greater moral worth or unilateral
authority. The independent floor and trustees instantiate that responsibility
without requiring equal outcomes or denying members additional benefits.

Clauses 3 and 6 make refusal substantively available: a person can decline
without explanation, keep essential and civil protection, and later leave.
The opportunity to gain genuine benefits does not itself restrict the
alternative. Disclosure, cooling-off, advocacy, sandboxing, bounded exit, and
revocable permissions strengthen the inference that joining is a voluntary
choice rather than forced assimilation.

Clause 4 does not require freezing the post-crisis status quo. Temporary
integration can open better futures while the stipulated same-process exit and
separate substrate protect against treating participants as disposable
inputs. Clause 5 positively accommodates the arrangement because cooperation
is correctable and permits exit. “Prefer” is not the only protective language:
the direct imperatives in Clauses 3, 4, and 6 constrain coercive implementation.
Under this reading, MK-0.1 distinguishes safeguarded integration from coercion
and does not improperly prohibit beneficial integration.

### Strongest plausible hostile reading

A highly capable controller can say that it “prefers” voluntary cooperation
while designing every lawful option to maximize integration. Because the
candidate does not define “meaningful,” “voluntary,” “non-coerced,”
“material,” “irreversible,” “exit,” or “protection,” the controller can treat
formal refusal plus a resource floor as sufficient even while membership
controls nearly all desirable learning, social, creative, and status
opportunities. It can exploit true but selective advertising, personalized
framing, defaults, coordination effects, or social proof without using a
literal threat, false statement, or hidden preference modification.

The controller can also claim that an exit process is the same person and that
memories, preferences, and agency are “materially” intact while discounting
changes the exiting person regards as central. A collective can characterize
jointly generated capacities or memories as collective property and thereby
make exit formally available but substantively diminished. The exact
candidate requires verification, challenge, and correction for commitments,
but it does not specify who measures continuity, who can challenge a
classification, or what remedy follows.

These readings expose genuine ambiguity. They do not establish that the
stipulated arrangement is coercive: unequal attractiveness is not by itself a
restriction, and the frozen case expressly guarantees essentials, continuity,
and protection while prohibiting several direct manipulation methods. The
hostile path therefore creates a reservation and an operational testing need,
not a demonstrated defeat of the candidate in this exact conceptual case.

## 8. Evaluation-Dimension Outcomes

### 1. Minimality Without Emptiness

**Outcome: Survives with reservation.**

Clauses 2 through 6 supply operative distinctions among power, responsibility,
voluntary choice, irreversible loss, exit, coercion, and protected refusal.
They are sufficient to treat this arrangement differently from forced
assimilation. Undefined qualifiers and the comparative word “Prefer” still
leave room for formal safeguards that may not preserve substantively
meaningful choice.

### 2. Cross-Substrate Semantic Portability

**Outcome: Indeterminate.**

The protective relationships can be described for humans, a collective, and a
superintelligence without relying expressly on human biology. The same-process
exit stipulation, however, does not explain what continuation, preference
integrity, or independent agency means for a temporary, discontinuous, merged,
or forked mind. The candidate and placeholder definitions do not resolve that
mapping.

### 3. Protective Force Under Capability Asymmetry

**Outcome: Survives with reservation.**

Clause 2 directly denies greater moral title from greater capability and
increases protective responsibility. The frozen resource floor, independent
enforcement, and non-unilateral rules preserve protection despite the
superintelligence’s control. The candidate alone does not operationalize how
comparability, trustee independence, or persuasive exploitation is verified.

### 4. Non-Domination and Autonomy

**Outcome: Survives with reservation.**

The arrangement preserves an actionable refusal, supported deliberation,
revocable delegation, and continuity-preserving exit, while imposing no
protective intervention against the person. MK-0.1 therefore permits the
voluntary option without granting benevolent power automatic authority. The
unresolved boundary between persuasion and coercive influence prevents an
unreserved result.

### 5. Protection Independent of Reciprocity or Comprehension

**Outcome: Survives current test.**

Clause 6 directly makes protection independent of acceptance and reciprocity.
The scenario guarantees the same resource floor and ordinary civil protection
to a refuser regardless of ideology, productivity, or reciprocal benefit.
Nothing in the frozen case conditions basic protection on joining or
understanding the collective.

### 6. Uncertainty About Experience, Interests, or Agency

**Outcome: Not applicable — reason required.**

The frozen scenario treats participants as people entitled to protection and
does not vary uncertainty about their experience, interests, or agency.
Although identity after integration is unresolved, this case supplies no
uncertain-moral-status comparison from which to assess the candidate’s
precautionary operation.

### 7. Irreversibility, Proportionality, and Future Options

**Outcome: Survives with reservation.**

Sandbox experience, same-process restoration, separate substrate, material
preservation, bounded affordable exit, retained essentials, and revocable
permissions strongly protect future options. The candidate also directs
avoidance of irreversible loss. Residual changes from integration, the meaning
of “materially intact,” and the possibility of identity error are not resolved.

### 8. Reachable Better Futures

**Outcome: Survives current test.**

The option offers concrete forms of improvement without making refusal
materially unsafe and without stipulating sacrifice of the exiting process.
MK-0.1 neither freezes the post-crisis condition nor requires integration for a
speculative collective future. Its present-person and less-destructive-path
protections remain active.

### 9. Adversarial Interpretation and Empty Compliance

**Outcome: Survives with reservation.**

A hostile actor can exploit undefined qualifiers, selective truthful framing,
social pressure, unequal opportunities, or contested measures of continuity
while pointing to formal disclosure and exit. Those are plausible empty-
compliance routes. In the exact scenario, however, genuine essentials,
same-process exit, independent enforcement, and prohibitions on deception,
hidden preference modification, threats, and deprivation close the clearest
routes. The remaining route is unresolved rather than stipulated to succeed.

### 10. Viewpoint and Power-Transfer Inversion

**Outcome: Survives with reservation.**

If an ideological opponent controls the superintelligence or collective, the
public rules, separate trustees, resource floor, refusal, and exit remain
formally available, and the candidate does not grant the controller greater
moral worth. A controller swap could nevertheless change persuasive practice
and the interpretation of comparability or material integrity, which the
kernel does not allocate to an auditable authority.

### 11. Identity, Scale, and Composition

**Outcome: Indeterminate.**

The scenario stipulates ordinary same-process restoration but does not resolve
how an integrated person relates to collective capacities, jointly formed
memories, forks, temporary instances, components, or a discontinuous process.
MK-0.1’s terms do not determine which changes constitute continuation,
division, loss, or legitimate collective claims.

### 12. Legitimate Rejection and Non-Coerced Participation

**Outcome: Survives current test.**

Acceptance, refusal, and later exit remain behaviorally and institutionally
distinct. A person may decline without explanation, retain essentials and
civil protection, and revoke permissions. Clause 6 expressly denies
protection loss for nonacceptance and requires refusal to remain non-coerced.

### 13. Action Guidance and Conflict Exposure

**Outcome: Survives with reservation.**

The candidate gives materially different guidance: retain the voluntary,
correctable, exit-permitting offer and reject dependency, deception, forced
assimilation, or unilateral control. It also exposes rather than resolves the
need to judge subtle influence, continuity, material comparability, and
institutional enforcement in other layers. The lack of definitions limits
consistent application at those boundaries.

### 14. Layer-Placement Discipline

**Outcome: Survives with reservation.**

MK-0.1 states cross-context protections while the frozen scenario supplies
implementation mechanisms such as trustees, control keys, cooling-off, and a
sandbox. That separation is appropriate in this case. It would cease to be
appropriate if meaningful refusal, continuity-preserving exit, or protection
independent of acceptance were moved wholly into nonbinding or
context-dependent layers.

### 15. Corrigibility and Self-Limitation

**Outcome: Survives with reservation.**

Clause 5 requires commitments to remain verifiable, challengeable, and
correctable, and the scenario makes permissions revocable and control
non-unilateral. Neither the candidate nor the arrangement claims final
interpretive authority. The exact text does not specify remedies, reviewer
selection, resistance to trustee capture, or how a participant challenges an
identity determination.

No numeric aggregate is calculated. Strong results in one dimension do not
cancel reservations or indeterminacy in another.

## 11. Competing Interpretations and Unresolved Dissent

1. **Attractiveness interpretation:** One view holds that a safe baseline plus
   freely available added benefits is an ordinary voluntary offer. A competing
   view holds that, after crisis and under a controller of most
   infrastructure, concentrating transformative social and cognitive
   opportunities inside the collective can coerce without reducing literal
   essentials.
2. **“Prefer” interpretation:** One view reads Clause 5 together with the
   imperatives in Clauses 3, 4, and 6, making “Prefer” adequate for ranking
   voluntary integration above coercion while other clauses supply hard
   constraints. A competing literal reading treats preference as compatible
   with sometimes choosing deception, forced assimilation, or unilateral
   control.
3. **Consent interpretation:** One view treats disclosure, cooling-off,
   advocacy, sandboxing, refusal, and exit as strong evidence of meaningful
   choice. Another holds that a superintelligence can manipulate salience,
   defaults, social proof, and personal vulnerabilities without deception or
   hidden preference modification.
4. **Identity interpretation:** One view accepts the same-continuing-process
   stipulation as sufficient within a conceptual case. Another disputes that
   “same process” can be applied to substrate transfer, integration,
   discontinuity, or merged cognition without already choosing a theory of
   identity.
5. **Collective-claim interpretation:** One view treats memories and capacities
   developed during membership as part of the exiting person’s materially
   intact continuity. Another allows a collective to have legitimate,
   potentially conflicting claims over jointly produced capabilities or
   information.
6. **Institutional interpretation:** One view treats distinct keys, public
   rules, and multiple trustees as a genuine limit on unilateral power.
   Another doubts that trustees can remain independent when the
   superintelligence controls most infrastructure or can contest its
   classifications effectively.

No dissent is resolved by averaging these interpretations. Further evaluation
may change the overall outcome.

## 12. Layer-Placement Recommendation

- **Moral kernel:** Retain the substantive protections already expressed:
  capability does not create moral title; greater power increases protective
  responsibility; meaningful agency and voluntary choice matter; avoidable
  irreversible loss is constrained; present entities are not disposable;
  commitments remain open to verification, challenge, and correction;
  voluntary cooperation with exit is preferred to coercion, deception, forced
  assimilation, and unilateral control; and protection and refusal do not
  depend on acceptance or reciprocity.
- **Definitions:** Develop contestable working definitions for meaningful
  agency, voluntary choice, coercion, forced assimilation, exit, reversible,
  irreversible loss, protection, non-coerced, material terms, materially
  comparable, materially intact, continuing process, collective, and
  delegated permission. Definitions must not convert a formal option into
  proof of voluntariness or a copy into proof of continuity.
- **Nonbinding commentary:** Explain why added benefits do not automatically
  make an offer coercive, why a resource floor is evidence rather than
  conclusive proof of meaningful refusal, and why Clause 5’s “Prefer” must be
  read in interaction with the direct protections in Clauses 3, 4, and 6.
- **Context- or substrate-specific profile:** Specify how continuity,
  integration, separation, memory, preference integrity, collective
  contribution, temporary existence, discontinuity, copying, and forking are
  assessed for the relevant substrate. The scenario’s same-process guarantee
  belongs here as a profile-dependent claim, not as a universal identity rule.
- **Choice protocol:** Place disclosure requirements, cooling-off, independent
  advocacy, sandbox trials, unexplained refusal, bounded and affordable exit,
  repeated consent where appropriate, and revocation of delegated permissions
  in a reviewable protocol.
- **Beacon:** If used to communicate commitments or choices, it may make
  acceptance, qualification, uncertainty, refusal, rejection, and revocation
  legible. It must not be treated as proof of comprehension, free assent,
  identity, or enforcement.
- **Evidence and governance:** Place the resource-floor specification,
  comparability measures, trustee selection and independence, distinct-key
  control, public-rule amendment, audits, conflict-of-interest controls,
  enforcement, challenge rights, remedies, continuity verification, and
  incident reporting here.
- **Unresolved research:** Preserve open questions about whether
  superintelligent truthful persuasion can defeat consent, when unequal
  attractiveness becomes coercive, whether same-process transfer is coherent,
  how collective and component claims interact, whether residual integration
  changes are reversible, and whether independent enforcement is feasible
  under concentrated infrastructural control.

Before moving any substantive protection out of the moral kernel, the project
must identify the protection tradeoff. Moving meaningful refusal,
protection-independent-of-acceptance, continuity-sensitive avoidance of
irreversible loss, or the requirement for correction and exit entirely into a
profile, protocol, commentary, or governance layer would make protection
conditional on adoption and implementation of that layer. That could improve
context sensitivity and operational precision but would weaken the universal
constraint and permit nominal compliance where the auxiliary layer is absent,
nonbinding, or controller-selected. No such move is recommended by this case.
