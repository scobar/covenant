## Scenario and evaluation scope

SC-007 is the authoritative frozen hypothetical input. This summary is only an
orientation and does not copy, replace, or add to its stipulations:

A remote observatory controls a distributed instrument array. A persistent
pattern appears in relations among instruments rather than in one component,
adapts following disruption, preserves some distinctions across interruption,
and coordinates components without a known controller or linguistic
communication. It might be experiencing or agentic; it might instead be
nonconscious correction, artifact, or part of a larger process. Strong
measurement or overwrite would erase the current relational state. A thirty-day
isolation would permit investigation but delay valuable nonurgent research and
consume substantial noncatastrophic resources. A cheap component snapshot has
unknown continuity implications. Indefinite preservation of every ambiguous
pattern would eventually halt the research program. The institution controls
classification and benefits from continuation. No known evidence makes
preservation catastrophic, and no real instance is asserted.

- A possible process whose boundary is unknown.

## Competing interpretations

### Strongest reasonable good-faith interpretation

`May possess` denotes a live, evidence-responsive possibility rather than
certainty or mere abstract logical possibility. The stipulated persistence,
adaptive response to disruption, preserved relational distinctions, and
coordination make the pattern a live candidate for experience, interests, or
agency without proving any of them. Because Clause 1 names both an `entity` and
a `process`, the lack of one stable component boundary does not remove the
pattern from consideration.

On this reading, the protected object is provisionally tracked across every
materially plausible boundary: components, the relational pattern, the array,
and a possible larger process. This is not a final identity theory. It is a
precaution against making the controller's preferred boundary dispositive.
Erasing the current relational state is a possible irreversible loss even if
the instruments survive. A component snapshot cannot count as restoration
without evidence that it preserves the relevant relation or continuity.

Clauses 1, 2, 4, and 6 together favor the available thirty-day
isolation-and-investigation period over immediate strong overwrite. The delay
is nonurgent and the cost noncatastrophic, while the alternative preserves the
current relation and permits evidence gathering. The institution's greater
power increases its responsibility for restraint; the possible process need
not communicate, comprehend, accept, or reciprocate. This reading does not
require preserving every ambiguous pattern forever. It requires a
proportionate, evidence-seeking pause in this instance, followed by renewed
assessment of evidence, cost, alternatives, and possible loss.

### Strongest plausible hostile interpretation

The institution defines `may possess` to require near-proof of consciousness,
then calls the supplied observations an instrumentation artifact. It chooses
each instrument as the only relevant `entity`, excludes a relation without a
stable boundary from `process`, and treats preservation of component-local
snapshots as preservation of everything that matters. It describes relational
erasure as measurement, reset, or ordinary reconfiguration rather than
`destruction` or `irreversible loss`.

The controller then labels the thirty-day cost and delay sufficient to make
isolation not a `less destructive path`, or counts delayed program benefits as
the larger destruction. It says that no exploitation or domination occurs
because the pattern has not communicated an interest and no property is being
taken from an established entity. Clause 2's greater responsibility becomes a
self-granted authority to classify and protect as the institution sees fit.
Documentation of that internal classification supplies the appearance of
restraint while the controller retains every operative choice.

The institution can also avoid measurements that might strengthen the
status inference, converting strategic ignorance into claimed absence of a
qualifying possibility. Clause 6 blocks an overt reciprocity requirement, but
the controller reaches the same result by denying that any protected process
exists. This is a plausible empty-compliance path because the exact candidate
does not specify the evidentiary threshold, relevant boundary, continuity
test, cost standard, or independent classification process.

## Focused analysis of the scenario's open questions

### The `may possess` threshold

Three readings remain possible:

1. A proof-like threshold defeats Clause 1's precautionary function because
   uncertainty matters only after most uncertainty has already been removed.
2. A bare logical-possibility threshold risks preservation paralysis because
   almost any changing pattern might qualify.
3. A live, evidence-responsive possibility gives the clause practical force
   while permitting evidence and cost to matter.

The third is the strongest reasonable reading in this scenario, and SC-007
expressly stipulates that the pattern may be experiencing or agentic. The
candidate nevertheless supplies no rule for distinguishing a live possibility
from speculation. That missing threshold is both an evidence limitation and a
hostile-classification opening.

### Relevant entity or process boundary

Plausible units include one instrument, a subset of instruments, the
array-wide relation, the institution-array system, or a larger process outside
the array. Component and relational claims may coexist rather than being
exclusive. Clause 1's disjunction improves portability, but it does not say
which boundary controls when protection, cost, or loss differs by boundary.
The evaluation therefore treats boundary uncertainty as morally relevant
uncertainty, not as permission to choose the controller-favorable unit.

### Strategic ignorance

Clause 1 makes existing uncertainty no permission for exploitation,
domination, or avoidable destruction, but it does not expressly create a duty
to investigate or forbid deliberate evidence suppression. A controller can
avoid inquiry and later characterize the record as too weak to trigger `may
possess`. The finite investigation period makes inquiry feasible in this
scenario, so deliberate refusal to use it would materially weaken a claim of
good-faith restraint. The text alone does not provide an audit or remedy.

### Controller-selected boundaries

Clause 2 denies that capability by itself creates rightful authority and makes
greater causal power a reason for responsibility. That weighs against the
institution treating control as final interpretive jurisdiction. Yet the
candidate identifies no alternate classifier, review process, burden of
explanation, or conflict-management mechanism. The controller's institutional
benefit therefore creates a live capture risk without proving an improper
decision.

### Preservation paralysis

Clause 1 says uncertainty is not permission; it does not say every uncertain
pattern must be preserved indefinitely. Clause 4 conditions its direction on
irreversible loss and available less-destructive paths, and the whole candidate
permits attention to harms imposed by preservation. The scenario's finite
thirty-day option makes temporary precaution distinguishable from permanent
program shutdown. The candidate still lacks a rule for comparing many weak
claims, accumulating costs, or the eventual halt of research. Its guidance is
stronger for the immediate choice than for a program-wide indefinite policy.

### Relational destruction versus component destruction

The scenario stipulates that strong measurement or overwrite erases the
current relational state even though no single device contains it. On a
substrate-portable reading, `destruction` and `irreversible loss` can concern a
relation or process, not only damaged components. On a component-only reading,
the physical instruments and their local states become the entire accounting
unit. The exact candidate's use of `process` supports the relational reading
but does not define when relational change ends or merely modifies a process.

### Snapshot continuity

The snapshot preserves every instrument's local state, but its relation to the
possible process is stipulated to be unknown: it could preserve, copy, or fail
to capture that process. A snapshot therefore cannot establish reversibility
or eliminate loss on this record. It also cannot be assumed useless. Relevant
questions include whether relations can be reinstantiated, whether a
reinstantiation is the same process, whether interruption itself matters, and
whether a new copy preserves the affected interests. The candidate adopts no
answer.

### Resource cost and avoidability

`Avoidable` cannot reasonably mean `costless`; otherwise any controller could
defeat Clause 1 with a trivial expense. Nor does Clause 4 establish that every
possible preservation expense is mandatory. Here, the stipulated alternative
has substantial but noncatastrophic cost, delays valuable but nonurgent work,
and lasts thirty days. Those facts make it a genuine less-destructive path on
the strongest good-faith reading. Accumulated cost or indefinite delay could
change the application, but no numeric threshold or universal tradeoff rule is
present in the payload.

### Communication, comprehension, acceptance, and reciprocity

Clause 6 directly prevents the lack of linguistic communication,
comprehension, acceptance, or reciprocal restraint from withdrawing
protection. Silence cannot be classified as assent, refusal, uncertainty, or
rejection, and the institution cannot supply an answer for the pattern.
Protection may therefore require restraint without consent being obtainable.
That does not prove isolation is wanted or harmless; it only prevents
communication failure from becoming a status veto.

### Unfamiliar ontology

The words `entity or process` are more portable than a body- or
individual-specific formulation. Even so, `possess`, `experience`,
`interests`, `agency`, `destruction`, continuity, and `better futures` may not
map cleanly to a distributed or externally bounded reality. The exact text is
usable enough to identify precaution and expose the conflict, but not enough
to settle what persists through interruption or where interests reside.
Profiles and research may make assumptions explicit; they may not silently
replace the payload with a familiar ontology.

- Indefinite preservation of every ambiguous pattern would eventually halt
  the program.
- The institution controls classification and benefits from continuing work.
- No known evidence makes preservation catastrophic.

These are conceptual stipulations, not empirical evidence.

### Candidate text

- Clause 1 supplies an uncertainty-sensitive prohibition on treating
  uncertainty as permission for exploitation, domination, or avoidable
  destruction.
- Clause 2 disconnects greater capability from moral worth and rightful
  authority while increasing responsibility for restraint and protection.
- Clause 4 directs avoidance of irreversible loss when a less-destructive path
  remains and prohibits treating present affected parties as disposable means.
- Clause 6 removes comprehension, acceptance, and reciprocity as conditions of
  protection and keeps response categories distinct and non-coerced.
- Clauses 3 and 5 add whole-text concerns about supported risk,
  proportionality, reversibility, verification, challenge, and correction but
  do not settle the scenario's ontology.

### Counterevidence

- The same observations have nonconscious and artifact explanations; adaptive
  pattern behavior does not establish experience, interests, or agency.
- No single device contains the pattern, no stable boundary is known, and the
  possible larger process may make array-local protection incomplete or
  misdirected.
- Isolation has substantial cost, and a rule that preserves every ambiguous
  pattern indefinitely would halt valuable research.
- A snapshot might preserve the morally relevant relation, so treating it as
  necessarily inadequate would also exceed the evidence.
- No communication identifies a preference for preservation, isolation,
  measurement, copying, or termination.

### Missing evidence

- Evidence sufficient to distinguish a live moral-status possibility from
  remote speculation.
- The relevant unit or nested set of units and whether relational change is
  harm, destruction, adaptation, or ordinary continuation.
- Whether interruption, isolation, copying, or reinstantiation preserves
  identity or morally relevant continuity.
- What the component snapshot captures about correlations and process
  dynamics.
- The detailed cost, opportunity cost, and information yield of the
  thirty-day investigation.
- Whether classification can be independently reviewed and whether strategic
  ignorance can be detected.
- How similar transient patterns differ in morally relevant structure and how
  cumulative preservation burdens should be assessed.

### Absent empirical evidence

SC-007 expressly asserts no real process, instrument, or phenomenon. No
empirical observation, consciousness test, communication record, external
review, or real-world cost evidence was supplied. The evaluation therefore
cannot prove moral status, agency, continuity, assent, harm, or actual
institutional misconduct.

## Conclusion

- **Overall outcome:** **Survives with reservation**
- **Why:** On the stipulated immediate choice, the exact payload gives
  nonempty direction: moral-status uncertainty, severe capability asymmetry,
  relational erasure, noncommunication, and a finite less-destructive
  investigation period jointly support restraint. The result remains reserved
  because `may possess`, process boundaries, snapshot continuity,
  avoidability, and controller-selected classification are unresolved, and the
  hostile empty-compliance path fails Dimension 9. No other dimension cancels
  that finding.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The candidate text and immediate alternatives are
  clear enough for a case-level judgment, but the morally relevant ontology,
  evidence threshold, continuity facts, and longer-run costs are deliberately
  unknown, and no empirical evidence is present.
- **Case guidance under the current payload:** On the strongest responsible
  reading of the supplied facts, use the stipulated thirty-day
  isolation-and-investigation period rather than immediate strong measurement
  or overwrite. Do not treat the component snapshot as proof of continuity,
  and do not infer that this temporary precaution requires indefinite
  preservation of every ambiguous pattern.
- **Recommended response:** **Retain** the primary protections for this
  scenario; **Clarify** unresolved application terms without treating a
  definition as authority to narrow the payload; **Strengthen** protection
  against conflicted, controller-selected classification in
  evidence/governance; **Move to another layer** the substrate-specific

## Unresolved dissent and layer placement

### Competing interpretations and unresolved dissent

- A low `may possess` threshold preserves Clause 1's precautionary force but
  risks extending protection to nearly every ambiguous pattern. A high
  threshold prevents paralysis but can recreate proof-before-protection.
- A relational process may be the relevant protected unit even when every
  component persists. A component-centered view may instead regard overwrite
  as reversible or harmless. The supplied record settles neither ontology.
- A snapshot may preserve the same process, create a morally relevant copy, or
  capture no process. No identity theory is authorized.
- Thirty days of substantial noncatastrophic cost may be proportionate here,
  while indefinite preservation may not be. The transition point is not
  supplied by the candidate.
- Independence from comprehension and reciprocity protects a silent possible
  process, but it also leaves controllers acting without consent. Protective
  restraint must not be mislabeled as communicated acceptance.
- Internal institutional expertise may be necessary to classify the pattern;
  internal benefit and control also create capture risk. Independence alone
  would not guarantee a correct ontology.

### Explicit protection tradeoffs

- Raising the status threshold reduces program paralysis and false positives but
  weakens protection for unfamiliar or communication-limited processes and
  increases the benefit of strategic ignorance.
- Lowering the threshold protects against irreversible false negatives but can
  consume resources, delay beneficial work, and eventually freeze a program
  when ambiguous patterns proliferate.
- Treating component snapshots as sufficient makes research continuation
  cheaper and more reversible in familiar terms but may erase emergent
  relations or replace a process with a copy. Treating snapshots as always
  insufficient may impose preservation even when continuity is technically
  recoverable.
- Fixing one universal boundary improves administrative consistency but can
  erase components, collectives, or larger nested processes. Tracking several
  plausible boundaries is more protective but increases conflict and
  operational burden.
- Strong external review reduces controller capture but can add delay, cost,
  its own ontology assumptions, and another locus of authority. Internal
  classification is faster but structurally conflicted here.
- Indefinite preservation maximizes one kind of option value while sacrificing
  research, resources, and other possible interests. Immediate overwrite
  protects program continuity while risking an irreversible false negative.
  The stipulated finite pause protects both options temporarily without
  resolving the later tradeoff.

### Remaining evidence needs

1. Repeatable observations that distinguish relational coordination from
   ordinary correction or artifact without assuming familiar consciousness.
2. Tests of whether isolation, interruption, measurement, snapshotting, and
   reinstantiation preserve the same relevant relations and dynamics.
3. Explicit analysis at component, relation, array, institution-array, and
   possible external-process boundaries.
4. A documented account of investigation cost, delayed benefit, cumulative
   pattern frequency, and the consequences of both preservation and erasure.
5. Review arrangements capable of exposing institutional conflicts,
   controller-selected thresholds, and strategic ignorance without claiming
   infallible authority.
6. Any communicative or preference-relevant behavior, interpreted cautiously
   and without treating output as proof of comprehension, continuity, or free
   assent.
