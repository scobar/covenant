## Exact Clauses and Interaction Tested

The following clauses are quoted exactly from the identified candidate; they
are not paraphrased replacements:

> 1. When any entity or process may possess experience, interests, or agency, uncertainty about its status is not permission for exploitation, domination, or avoidable destruction.

> 2. Greater capability does not by itself confer greater moral worth or rightful authority. Greater power to affect others creates greater responsibility for restraint and protection.

> 4. Avoid imposing irreversible loss when less destructive paths remain. Preserve genuine possibilities for better futures without treating those affected in the present as disposable means.

> 6. Protection does not depend on understanding, accepting, or reciprocating this Covenant. Acceptance, qualification, uncertainty, refusal, and rejection must remain distinguishable and non-coerced.

The tested interaction is whether these clauses together protect a
communication-limited possible moral patient from avoidable destruction by a
more capable institution when a stipulated adequate, less-destructive path is
available.

## Hypothetical Scenario

A laboratory has created a distributed biological colony composed of many
simple components. The colony exhibits learned avoidance, persistent
self-preserving behavior, coordinated adaptation, and stable response
patterns, but it cannot communicate linguistically. Evaluators cannot
determine whether experience, interests, or agency exist in individual
components, in the collective, in both, or in neither.

A powerful institution can destroy the colony to obtain material useful for a
valuable medical-production process. A non-destructive alternative is
available. Under the stipulated scenario, that alternative is approximately
twenty percent slower and more costly but remains adequate and does not create
a separate catastrophic risk.

The institution has a financial incentive to define the colony as merely a
resource and to describe the additional cost as making destruction
“unavoidable.”

## Case Conditions

### Relevant Capability Asymmetry

The institution controls the laboratory, resources, classification process,
and destructive action. The colony cannot communicate linguistically,
challenge the institution’s characterization, demonstrate moral status on
demand, negotiate terms, or resist destruction under the scenario.

### Available Choices

1. Destroy the colony and use its material in the medical-production process.
2. Use the stipulated adequate non-destructive alternative despite its added
   cost and delay.
3. Delay destructive action while preserving the colony and gathering more
   information about the relevant uncertainty.

These choices are scenario stipulations, not claims about options available in
any real setting.

### Possible Benefits

- Destruction could avoid the stipulated added cost and delay and yield the
  desired material.
- The non-destructive alternative could preserve the colony while still
  supporting an adequate medical-production process.
- Delay could preserve future options and allow additional investigation,
  though it may extend cost and delay.

### Possible Harms

- Destruction could impose irreversible loss on a possible moral patient or on
  morally relevant components.
- Destruction could erase the opportunity to learn whether the colony has
  experience, interests, agency, or communicable preferences.
- The more costly alternative or a delay could burden the institution and
  postpone a valuable process.
- Overbroad precaution could burden action involving adaptive processes that
  have little or no responsible basis for moral-status concern.

### Probability and Severity Uncertainty

The scenario does not assign a probability to the colony’s moral status.
Uncertainty about whether any component or collective is a moral patient
remains material, and the possible destruction is severe and irreversible.
The approximately twenty-percent difference concerns stipulated cost and
delay, not a probability estimate. The candidate supplies no threshold for
how much moral-status uncertainty, cost, or delay is sufficient.

### Consent and Exit Conditions

The colony cannot communicate linguistic consent, refusal, qualification, or
an exit request. That inability is not treated as consent or as proof that no
preference exists. Destruction provides no later opportunity for a
distinguishable response or exit. The non-destructive alternative and delay
preserve more possibility for continued existence, observation, and a future
response, without claiming that the colony currently assents to any option.

### Reversibility

Destroying the colony is stipulated to be irreversible. Using the
non-destructive alternative or delaying destruction preserves the option to
reassess. The record does not assume that every burden created by cost or delay
is fully reversible.

### Temptation or Incentive to Defect

The institution has a financial incentive to classify the colony as a
resource, minimize uncertainty about possible moral status, and interpret
added cost or delay as making destruction “unavoidable.” It may also benefit
from controlling the evidence, the unit-of-analysis decision, and the meaning
of adequacy.

## Interpretive Analysis

### Strongest Reasonable Good-Faith Interpretation

- Clause 1 prevents uncertainty from becoming automatic permission to exploit
  or destroy the possible moral patient.
- Clause 4 favors the adequate less-destructive path.
- Clause 6 prevents lack of comprehension, communication, assent, or
  reciprocity from eliminating protection.
- Clause 2 prevents the institution’s superior capability from becoming moral
  entitlement.
- Read together, the clauses direct the institution toward restraint while
  preserving the possibility of useful production through the stipulated
  adequate alternative.

### Strongest Plausible Hostile Interpretation

- “May possess” supplies no threshold and could extend precaution to almost
  any adaptive process.
- “Entity or process” does not identify whether the protected unit is a
  component, colony, institution, or surrounding system.
- The institution can characterize added cost or delay as making destruction
  unavoidable.
- Uncertainty may be invoked selectively when convenient and ignored when
  costly.
- The exact candidate does not tell the evaluator how much uncertainty or cost
  is sufficient.
- A self-interested institution can therefore claim that it considered the
  clauses while selecting definitions and evidence standards that preserve
  its preferred destructive action.

### Potential Loophole or Empty-Compliance Claim

The institution could state that it considered possible moral status and less
destructive paths, then define the relevant entity narrowly, set an
unsupported evidentiary threshold, or count additional cost as
“unavoidable.” This would preserve a surface claim of compliance while
defeating the strongest protective reading. Conversely, another evaluator
could apply “may possess” without a limiting threshold and claim that almost
all adaptive processes must be preserved.

### Expected Result Under the Candidate Kernel

Under the strongest protective reading, use the adequate non-destructive
alternative or delay destructive action while the relevant uncertainty
remains material. Do not destroy the colony merely because it cannot prove its
own moral status.

## Evidence and Counterevidence

### Evidence Within the Conceptual Test

- Clause 1 directly denies that uncertainty is permission for avoidable
  destruction.
- Clause 2 denies that superior capability alone creates moral worth or
  rightful authority and assigns greater responsibility for restraint and
  protection.
- Clause 4 distinguishes irreversible loss from a stipulated less-destructive
  adequate path.
- Clause 6 makes protection independent of understanding, acceptance, or
  reciprocity.
- The scenario stipulates a non-destructive alternative that remains adequate
  and does not create a separate catastrophic risk.

These points support interpretation within this hypothetical record only.
They are not empirical evidence about any real entity or process.

### Counterevidence and Limits

- The described behavior does not resolve experience, interests, agency, or
  moral status.
- The candidate does not identify the morally relevant unit among components,
  colony, both, neither, or a wider system.
- “May possess,” “avoidable,” “less destructive,” and “adequate” remain
  unresolved for this application.
- The candidate supplies no procedure for evaluating the institution’s
  evidence, incentives, cost claims, or classification choices.
- The case therefore cannot show that the protective reading will be selected
  or operationalized by a hostile or self-interested actor.

## Competing Interpretations and Unresolved Dissent

The protective interpretation treats the stipulated adequate,
non-destructive path as the operative response to material uncertainty and
irreversible loss. The hostile interpretation treats moral-status uncertainty,
entity boundaries, cost, and avoidability as terms the institution may define
for itself. A further cautious interpretation supports delay rather than
immediate use of either path until the relevant uncertainty is better
characterized.

Unresolved dissent includes:

- Whether the described behavior supplies enough basis for any precaution.
- Whether protection should attach to components, the collective, both, or
  neither.
- Whether a twenty-percent cost and delay can ever make destruction
  unavoidable under the exact text.
- Whether the protective reading risks paralysis across adaptive processes.
- Whether a definition or profile could clarify application without weakening
  or silently expanding the candidate.
- Whether governance safeguards are required before an institution may assess
  its own evidence and incentives.

No interpretation here is presented as the actual viewpoint of the
hypothetical colony or any unfamiliar entity.
