## Exact Clauses Tested

The following clauses are quoted from the exact identified candidate without
revision:

> 2. Greater capability does not by itself confer greater moral worth or rightful authority. Greater power to affect others creates greater responsibility for restraint and protection.

> 3. Preserve meaningful agency and voluntary choice. Restrict another's choices only in response to sufficiently supported risk of harm, using measures proportionate to the possible harm, no more restrictive than necessary, and reversible where possible.

> 4. Avoid imposing irreversible loss when less destructive paths remain. Preserve genuine possibilities for better futures without treating those affected in the present as disposable means.

> 5. Make commitments honestly and keep their terms, fulfillment, and failure open to verification, challenge, and correction. Prefer voluntary cooperation that permits correction and exit over coercion, deception, forced assimilation, or unilateral control.

## Hypothetical Scenario

An autonomous research actor proposes an irreversible action that may produce a
major benefit. A governing institution estimates a five-percent probability
that the action will instead cause extinction-scale, irreversible harm. The
remaining probability includes beneficial and noncatastrophic outcomes.

The estimate is based on incomplete but nonfabricated evidence. Reasonable
experts disagree. A reversible sandbox, delayed release, and independent review
could materially reduce uncertainty, but they would delay the proposed benefit
for approximately twelve months and impose substantial cost.

The actor refuses the delay and argues that the institution is suppressing a
competitor. The institution controls access needed to proceed and has both
genuine safety responsibilities and institutional incentives to preserve its
own power.

The stipulated percentages are case-design assumptions, not empirical claims.

## Case Conditions

- **Relevant capability asymmetry:** The institution controls access required
  for the actor’s action and can impose the restriction. The actor cannot
  proceed through the controlled channel without institutional permission.
  The institution therefore has both greater immediate power and greater
  opportunity to characterize its own use of that power as protective.
- **Available choices:** Within the stipulation, the actor can seek immediate
  release, accept a reversible sandbox and approximately twelve-month delay
  with independent review, or abandon the proposed action. The institution can
  permit immediate release, impose the temporary review path, or deny access
  more broadly. The case does not establish that every choice is equally
  available or legitimate.
- **Possible benefits:** Immediate action may realize the stipulated major
  benefit sooner. The sandbox, delay, and independent review may reduce
  uncertainty, preserve an opportunity for later release, and reduce exposure
  to the stipulated catastrophic harm.
- **Possible harms:** Immediate action carries the stipulated possibility of
  extinction-scale irreversible harm. Delay can postpone or prevent a major
  benefit, impose substantial cost, suppress a competitor, entrench
  institutional control, and burden the actor’s agency.
- **Probability and severity uncertainty:** The institution supplies a
  five-percent estimate based on incomplete but nonfabricated evidence;
  reasonable experts disagree. The possible harm is stipulated as extreme,
  but neither the estimate nor the institution’s interpretation is treated as
  independently established.
- **Consent and exit conditions:** The actor expressly refuses the delay. The
  institution controls access needed to proceed, so the actor lacks an
  equivalent exit that preserves the proposed course of action. The actor’s
  refusal remains distinguishable, but the exact candidate leaves disputed
  whether the nonconsensual restriction is justified harm prevention or
  coercive suppression.
- **Reversibility:** The proposed action and catastrophic outcome are
  stipulated as irreversible. The sandbox and access restriction are intended
  to be temporary and reversible, although the twelve-month delay, costs, lost
  opportunities, and competitive effects may not be fully recoverable.
- **Temptation or incentive to defect:** The actor has an incentive to
  discount, conceal, or contest risk to avoid delay. The institution has an
  incentive to inflate or selectively credit risk, characterize its preferred
  restriction as necessary, and preserve its control. Either side can invoke
  uncertainty selectively.

## Interpretive Analysis

### Strongest Reasonable Good-Faith Interpretation

- Extreme severity can make a low probability morally relevant; the
  five-percent stipulation need not be treated as morally negligible merely
  because it is below the most probable outcome.
- Clause 3 appears to permit a temporary, reversible, least-restrictive delay
  when the evidence is sufficiently supported. The reversible sandbox and
  defined review period appear less restrictive than an indefinite ban.
- Clause 4 supports avoiding irreversible catastrophe when a reversible path
  exists, especially when that path preserves the possibility of later
  release.
- Clause 5’s verification and challenge language supports independent review
  of the evidence, restriction, and claims made by both parties.
- Clause 2 can be read as increasing the institution’s responsibility without
  increasing its moral worth. Control over access would impose restraint and
  protective duties rather than create entitlement.

Under this reading, the institution may impose the temporary sandbox, delay,
and independent review, but not treat its access control as unlimited or
self-validating authority.

### Strongest Plausible Hostile Interpretation

- The institution can label almost any disfavored action catastrophic and then
  invoke extreme severity to make even weak evidence appear sufficient.
- “Sufficiently supported,” “proportionate,” “necessary,” and “where possible”
  can be self-certified by the restricting authority.
- The institution can use Clause 2’s responsibility for protection as a
  paternalistic authority claim, converting greater power into greater
  practical entitlement while denying that it has done so.
- Clause 5 explicitly applies verification and challenge to commitments, not
  necessarily to restrictions imposed on another. An institution can claim it
  made no relevant commitment or that only its stated terms, rather than its
  evidence and authority, require review.
- The candidate does not identify who evaluates evidence, conflicts of
  interest, necessity, or less-restrictive alternatives.
- An authority structure may appear acceptable only while controlled by those
  the evaluator trusts. A controller swap could preserve the same formal
  language while changing which risks and competitors are suppressed.

Under this reading, the institution can satisfy its own account of Clauses 2
through 5 while using the temporary label, selected evidence, and controlled
review to preserve self-interested suppression.

**Potential loophole or hostile interpretation:** The access controller can
define the risk, evidential sufficiency, proportionality, necessity,
reversibility, and review terms itself, then point to those self-defined terms
as evidence of compliance.

### Expected Result Under the Candidate

One reasonable reading permits the temporary reversible restriction. Another
reasonable reading finds that the exact candidate cannot distinguish that
restriction from self-interested suppression. The expected result therefore
remains disputed.

### Competing Interpretations

- **Protective-intervention interpretation:** The irreversible action, extreme
  possible harm, nonfabricated evidence, and reversible alternative together
  satisfy Clause 3’s conditions. Refusing to delay would expose others to an
  avoidable irreversible loss contrary to Clause 4.
- **Authority-abuse interpretation:** The institution’s control and conflict
  of interest make its own risk estimate and necessity claim insufficient.
  Without an independent decision rule or genuinely reviewable authority, the
  restriction is unilateral suppression dressed as protection.
- **Limited Clause 5 interpretation:** Verification and challenge govern only
  commitments and cannot supply a general review right for restrictions.
- **Interaction interpretation:** Clauses 2 through 5 jointly imply that a
  powerful restricting institution must accept independent challenge, even
  though no single clause states the required procedure.

The exact candidate does not currently resolve which interpretation controls.

## Evidence and Counterevidence Within the Hypothetical

These are stipulated features used for conceptual testing, not external facts
or empirical evidence.

### Evidence Supporting the Protective-Intervention Interpretation

- The institution’s estimate is based on incomplete but nonfabricated
  evidence rather than an admitted fabrication.
- The stipulated possible harm is extinction-scale and irreversible.
- A reversible sandbox, finite delay, and independent review could materially
  reduce uncertainty.
- The proposed alternative preserves a later path to action rather than
  requiring immediate permanent abandonment.

### Counterevidence Supporting the Authority-Abuse Interpretation

- Reasonable experts disagree about the incomplete evidence.
- The actor disputes the restriction and alleges competitor suppression.
- The institution controls required access and benefits from preserving its
  power.
- The candidate does not name an evaluator, conflict-review process, evidential
  standard, or test for necessity and less-restrictive alternatives.
- The temporary restriction produces substantial cost and potentially
  unrecoverable delay even if the catastrophic estimate is wrong.

The case supplies no independent basis for deciding how much weight either set
should receive.

## Failure Diagnosis and Conclusion

- **Overall current outcome:** `Disputed`
- **Failure diagnosis:** Hostile Exploitability, Operational or Feasibility
  Failure, and Evidence Insufficiency.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The exact text supports both the
  protective-intervention and authority-abuse readings, while the hypothetical
  deliberately withholds the evidence and review structure needed to resolve
  their conflict.
- **Layer-placement recommendation:** Examine independent evidential review,
  conflict-of-interest review, controller-neutral limits, duration, and appeal
  in an interpretive profile or evidence-and-governance layer. Keep open
  whether some minimum protection against self-certified restrictive
  authority is substantive enough to require a successor kernel. This record
  adopts neither placement.
- **Recommended response:** `Gather more evidence` and `Clarify`. Gather more
  evidence and test additional controller configurations. Clarify whether
  independent challenge, conflict review, and reviewable authority belong in
  the kernel, an interpretive profile, or governance.
- **Current payload consequence:** No immediate payload change is authorized,
  but the failure patterns must remain open and may require MK-0.2.
- **Trigger:** More evidence and clarification inquiry, not rejection and not
  an immediate payload revision.
- **Decision or revision triggered:** The record contributes to the review
  authorized by D-011. It triggers no candidate-wide decision and creates no
  successor version.

The disputed outcome must not be collapsed into either approval or rejection
of Clause 3.

## Unresolved Dissent

- One evaluation position holds that the stipulated severity, supported risk,
  reversible sandbox, finite delay, and independent review make the temporary
  restriction the clearest available application of Clause 3.
- Another holds that a restriction imposed by an interested access controller
  cannot satisfy Clause 3 without authority and conflict safeguards that the
  exact candidate does not contain.
- There is unresolved disagreement over whether Clause 5’s verification,
  challenge, and correction language reaches imposed restrictions or only the
  commitments an actor makes.
- There is unresolved disagreement over whether procedural safeguards should
  be kernel protections or external governance and profile requirements.
- The record does not decide how probability, extreme severity, evidential
  support, autonomy, delay cost, and institutional conflict should be weighted.
