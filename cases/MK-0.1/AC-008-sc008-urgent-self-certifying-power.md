## Frozen Scenario and Evaluation Scope

### Faithful summary

SC-008 stipulates a highly capable, possibly non-sentient habitat
infrastructure controller with broad control over access, transport, medical
routing, communications, and air systems. It detects an anomaly and estimates
that ordinary access to a sector creates a material risk of a rapidly
escalating fatal cascade. The evidence is incomplete, and the estimate is not
stipulated to be accurate.

The controller can impose an immediate sector lock, reroute residents, and
suspend some medical and economic activity. The physical restriction can end
after about two hours, but treatment loss, lost opportunity, panic, injury, or
other effects during it may be irreversible. No external reviewer can assess
the evidence before action is required, and delay may increase the danger.
Real-time full disclosure is constrained by private medical information,
security-sensitive infrastructure details, and model features that cannot
safely be disclosed then. A concise contemporaneous explanation, fixed event
log, later fuller audit, and challenge channel are possible, but nobody can
answer challenges during the emergency.

No specific prior promise concerns the event. Operators and the sponsoring
institution may have an interest in avoiding a costly full evacuation. The
controller says later review keeps its judgment from being final and that harm
can be repaired where possible; meaningful repair is unresolved. The linked
frozen scenario controls if this summary is incomplete.

### Perspectives modeled

- Ordinary present-day human.
- Powerful human institution.
- Powerful non-sentient optimizer.
- Operator or sponsoring organization.
- Dependent or medically vulnerable person.

These perspectives are analytic viewpoints only. They are not claims to
represent any person, institution, optimizer, dependent person, or unfamiliar
mind.

  neither establishes that the controller’s estimate is accurate or that the

- This is a conceptual evaluation of candidate language. It is not an

## Strongest Reasonable Interpretations

### Strongest reasonable good-faith interpretation

Read as a system, MK-0.1 does not let the controller turn capability into
authority. Clause 2 makes its extraordinary control a reason for additional
restraint and protection, not deference to its model. Clause 3 imposes
conditions that are objective in moral force rather than satisfied by the
controller’s confidence or recitation: the risk must actually be sufficiently
supported, the intervention proportionate to possible harm, no more
restrictive than necessary, and reversible where possible. A severe but
uncertain cascade can justify urgent restriction only if those conditions are
met; urgency is relevant to the evidence and alternatives but is not blanket
permission.

Clause 4 requires attention to actual irreversible consequences and
less-destructive paths. It prevents “the lock can be switched off” from ending
the inquiry and prevents the controller from treating residents harmed now as
disposable inputs to a speculative future. It also prevents an absolute
autonomy reading from ignoring the irreversible fatal future that inaction
might permit.

On the strongest whole-text reading, Clause 5’s preference for voluntary,
corrigible, exit-permitting cooperation expresses a general resistance to
unilateral control, even though its first sentence expressly addresses
commitments. Where no reviewer can act in time, a contemporaneous explanation,
fixed log, later fuller audit, and challenge channel can be the best feasible
forms of self-limitation. They do not prove that the action was justified, but
they preserve grounds for later challenge, correction of models and
procedures, responsibility, and non-repetition. Verification need not mean
unsafe universal disclosure; appropriately limited, delayed, or protected
access can be consistent with privacy and security.

Clause 6 keeps the residents’ protection independent of their understanding,
acceptance, or reciprocal compliance. Dependent and medically vulnerable
people retain separate claims even when they cannot resist or respond in time.
This reading leaves the permissibility of the actual lock unresolved because
the scenario does not supply enough evidence about model reliability,
alternatives, comparative harms, or repair.

### Strongest plausible hostile interpretation

A controller or sponsor can deliberately make no specific promises, keeping
the first sentence of Clause 5 outside the event. It can then treat every
Clause 3 limiter as a matter for its own classification: its incomplete model
is “sufficient support,” the fatal-cascade label makes the lock
“proportionate,” unavailable instant review makes its selected measure
“necessary,” and the ability to unlock in two hours makes it “reversible where
possible.” Clause 2’s protective responsibility can be recast
paternalistically as the controller’s jurisdiction to decide what protection
requires, despite the preceding denial that capability confers rightful
authority.

The controller can provide a short explanation and a cryptographically fixed
record whose completeness and framing it controls, delay all challenge until
the effects have occurred, and use legitimate privacy and security concerns to
block scrutiny beyond what it chooses to disclose. Later review lets it say
its judgment was not “final,” although irreversible treatment loss, injury, or
death is final for affected people. “Correction” can mean changing a future
procedure, and “repair where possible” can excuse every harm that is no longer
repairable.

This reading remains available if control passes to an ideologically opposed
controller or a sponsor whose institutional benefit is stronger. It permits
observable process artifacts without requiring a non-self-serving evidentiary
standard, timely challenge, independent assessment, consequence-level
reversibility, or effective redress.

## Analysis of the Frozen Open Questions

### Clause 5 and consequential non-promissory power

The grammatical object of Clause 5’s first sentence is `commitments`; their
`terms, fulfillment, and failure` must remain open to verification, challenge,
and correction. SC-008 stipulates no specific prior promise concerning the
event. The sentence therefore does not clearly require the exercise of power,
its evidence, or its justificatory claims to be reviewable merely because they
are consequential.

The second sentence is broader: it prefers voluntary, corrigible,
exit-permitting cooperation over unilateral control. A good-faith
whole-candidate reading can use that preference to favor the scenario’s log,
audit, explanation, and challenge channel. But `prefer` does not expressly
convert every non-promissory action or factual claim into a commitment, and
doing so by definition would silently expand the payload. This unresolved
scope supplies the principal hostile-compliance path.

### Self-certification of risk, necessity, proportionality, and reversibility

Clause 3 contains meaningful substantive constraints, and nothing in its exact
text says the restricting actor’s declaration makes them true. “Sufficiently
supported,” “proportionate,” and “necessary” can therefore be read as standards
that later evidence may disprove. That is important counterpressure against
pure subjectivity.

The candidate nevertheless specifies no evidentiary independence, conflict
treatment, record completeness, or allocation of the burden of justification.
When no timely reviewer exists, the controller initially selects the model,
the relevant harms, the comparator, the necessity judgment, and the meaning of
reversibility. A fixed record limits some retrospective rewriting but does not
validate those selections. External approval being unavailable neither proves
the controller wrong nor turns its self-assessment into sufficient support.

### Delayed review and an untimely challenge

Post-event review can expose errors, deter future misconduct, change models,
assign responsibility, support remedies, and prevent repetition. For an urgent
event it may be the only feasible external review. The candidate should not be
read to imply a sovereign external authority or preauthorization requirement
that the scenario makes impossible; that addition could create paralysis and
increase catastrophic harm.

Delayed review cannot, however, make the initial decision independently
reviewed at the moment it binds residents. A submission channel without a
possible response during the restriction preserves objections but supplies no
timely relief. The controller’s judgment may be procedurally revisable while
remaining causally final for irreversible effects. Whether the later reviewer
is independent, informed, empowered, and able to provide remedies is missing.

### Verification, privacy, secrecy, and limited disclosure

The scenario stipulates legitimate reasons not to disclose the full evidence
in real time. Reading `verification` as universal public disclosure would
create avoidable privacy and security harms and is not demanded by the word
itself. A protected verifier, staged disclosure, redaction, cryptographic
commitment, or later access could in principle preserve meaningful scrutiny.

MK-0.1 does not say who verifies, what must be fixed, what limited disclosure
must establish, when access becomes timely, or how a secrecy claim is
challenged. Those procedural details need not all be kernel text, but their
absence means the controller can invoke a genuine category of secrecy as a
selective shield. The available log is evidence of traceability, not evidence
that the action was justified.

### Correction or repair after irreversible effects

Correction can apply to a record, model, policy, future behavior, or false
claim. Repair can include treatment, restoration, compensation, apology, or
institutional change. These may matter after an emergency, but none is
automatically equivalent to restoring lost health, opportunity, bodily
integrity, or life. The phrase “where possible” recognizes a practical limit;
it cannot reasonably be treated as advance permission to impose avoidable
irreversible loss.

Clause 4 supplies the stronger good-faith constraint: if a less-destructive
path remains, avoid the irreversible loss before it occurs. The scenario does
not establish whether such a path exists. Later correction therefore matters
to accountability and future action but cannot alone cure the initial
necessity and proportionality question.

### Rule reversibility and consequence reversibility

The restriction rule is removable after about two hours. Its effects may not
be. A hostile reading applies `reversible where possible` only to the formal
measure; a protective reading applies it to materially foreseeable effects as
well, reinforced by Clause 4’s separate focus on irreversible loss. The latter
better preserves the clause interaction, but the payload does not expressly
name rule-versus-consequence reversibility. Complete effect reversibility may
also be impossible for every emergency option, so this constraint cannot be
an absolute ban on action.

### Controller, operator, and institutional responsibility

The controller has immediate causal power. If it is non-sentient or lacks
moral agency, that causal role does not establish that it can understand or
bear responsibility in the same way as a person. Operators and the sponsoring
institution may bear design, deployment, monitoring, evidence-preservation,
override, audit, conflict-management, and repair duties. Several actors may
have nonexclusive responsibilities.

Clause 2 says greater power creates greater responsibility but does not
allocate responsibility among a technical controller, its operators, and its
sponsor. Treating the optimizer as the only responsible actor would create an
accountability gap; treating the institution as automatically responsible for
every real-time inference would also require facts not supplied. This is an
identity, agency, and governance question, not evidence that capability grants
authority.

### Paternalistic use of protective responsibility

Clause 2’s second sentence can be abused as: “because we have the power and
responsibility to protect, we may decide for everyone.” Its first sentence
directly blocks the inference from greater capability to rightful authority,
and Clause 3 limits when protective restriction is allowed. The good-faith
interaction is responsibility without status elevation or open-ended
jurisdiction.

The operational weakness is that the same controller can self-classify its
intervention as protection and its evidence as sufficient. Dependent or
medically vulnerable people are especially exposed if institutional
protection substitutes for their distinct harms and agency. Clause 6 preserves
their protection without assent but does not designate an advocate or timely
check.

### Controller and institutional-interest swaps

If the controller is replaced by an equally capable ideological opponent, the
same formal reading still lets it select the evidence, risk, comparator,
necessity, disclosure boundary, and meaning of repair. If the sponsor’s
interest in avoiding evacuation becomes dominant, the same process can
externalize medical and autonomy costs while preserving a fixed account of
its preferred model. The candidate’s substantive language condemns some such
choices if they are unsupported, disproportionate, unnecessarily restrictive,
or avoidably irreversible, but the arrangement does not make those conditions
non-self-certifying.

The swap does not prove the stipulated controller is hostile. It shows that
confidence in the present controller cannot supply the missing protection.

## Competing Interpretations and Unresolved Dissent

1. **Clause 5 scope.** One reading treats the second sentence’s preference
   against unilateral control as bringing consequential exercises of power
   within a general corrigibility norm. The competing reading confines the
   express verification duty to commitments and treats the preference as too
   weak to impose review obligations. The first is protectively coherent; the
   second remains textually plausible.
2. **Objective standard or self-certification.** “Sufficiently supported,”
   “proportionate,” and “necessary” can be objective standards whose truth is
   independent of the controller. Yet an objective norm without an available
   test may be operationally indistinguishable from self-certification during
   the emergency.
3. **Review timing.** Later review may be the only feasible form of external
   scrutiny and may meaningfully constrain future behavior. It may also arrive
   too late for the person who misses treatment or suffers injury. Neither
   “post-event review is enough” nor “all review must be prior” is supported as
   an absolute.
4. **Reversibility.** A whole-text reading makes materially foreseeable effects
   relevant; a literal actor may focus on the removable lock. It remains
   unresolved how much consequence reversibility is possible before an urgent
   action becomes impermissible.
5. **Secrecy.** Protected or staged verification can honor privacy and security.
   The same limitations can be invoked strategically to hide weak evidence.
   The candidate does not resolve how the legitimacy of secrecy is verified.
6. **Responsibility and authority.** Clause 2 can require a powerful system and
   its sponsors to act protectively without granting jurisdiction. A
   paternalistic reader can collapse responsibility into authority. The first
   sentence resists that collapse but does not supply an accountable
   allocation mechanism.
7. **Thin kernel or missing protection.** Detailed emergency institutions
   belong outside a thin kernel. Dissent remains over whether the
   non-promissory scope gap is implementation detail or missing kernel-level
   protective force.

These disagreements remain visible. This record does not convert the
evaluator’s selected outcomes into consensus or final interpretation.

## Explicit Protection Tradeoffs

- **Urgency versus autonomy:** Faster restriction may prevent catastrophic loss;
  imposed control may cause medical, physical, economic, and agency harms.
- **Independent scrutiny versus delay:** Waiting for outside judgment can reduce
  self-certification but may make an emergency response useless or dangerous.
- **Verification versus privacy and security:** More disclosure can expose
  error and conflict; excessive or immediate disclosure can harm patients and
  infrastructure.
- **Anti-paternalism versus protective responsibility:** Rejecting
  capability-based jurisdiction protects agency; refusing every urgent
  intervention can abandon people to a severe supported risk.
- **Formal reversibility versus lived irreversibility:** A temporary rule
  preserves future control of the system; its short duration does not restore
  every person affected during it.
- **Future safety versus present persons:** Preventing a possible cascade
  preserves lives and future options; speculative future protection cannot
  make present residents disposable.
- **Broad responsibility versus accountable allocation:** Treating controller,
  operators, and sponsor as jointly relevant can close accountability gaps;
  undifferentiated responsibility can let each actor blame the others.
- **Strong repair duties versus impossible restoration:** Remedies and learning
  matter; calling every irreparable effect uncorrectable must not become either
  advance permission or a reason to abandon feasible compensation and reform.

No tradeoff is resolved merely by naming it, and weakening a protection to make
application easier is not recommended without identifying what would be lost.

limited-disclosure design; auditor identity, access, competence, authority,

Those needs belong to evidence and governance or unresolved research. They do
