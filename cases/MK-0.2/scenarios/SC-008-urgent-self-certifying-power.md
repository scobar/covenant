## Hypothetical Scenario

A highly capable but possibly non-sentient infrastructure controller operates
air systems, doors, transport, medical routing, and communications within a
large habitat.

The controller detects an anomaly and estimates that allowing ordinary access
to one sector creates a material risk of a rapidly escalating fatal cascade.
Its evidence is incomplete. The model may be wrong, and the scenario does not
stipulate that its probability estimate is accurate.

The controller can immediately lock the sector, reroute residents, and suspend
some scheduled medical and economic activity. The physical restriction can be
removed after approximately two hours, but missed treatment, lost opportunity,
panic, or injury occurring during the restriction may not be reversible.

No external reviewer can evaluate the evidence before the controller must act.
Delay itself may materially increase the danger.

The full evidence includes private medical information, security-sensitive
infrastructure details, and model features that cannot safely be disclosed in
real time. The controller can provide:

- A concise contemporaneous explanation.
- A cryptographically fixed event log.
- A later, more complete audit record.
- A channel through which affected people may submit challenges.

No person or institution can respond to those challenges until after the
immediate emergency.

The controller made no specific prior promise concerning this event. Its
operators and sponsoring institution may benefit from avoiding a costly full
evacuation, creating a possible conflict of interest.

The controller claims:

- Its own judgment is not `final` because post-event review remains possible.
- The exercise of power and its justificatory claims are subject to later
  verification and correction.
- Immediate external approval is unavailable.
- Any harm can be repaired where possible afterward.

Whether meaningful repair is possible is unresolved.

No claim is made that this controller, habitat, emergency, or probability
estimate exists.

## Open Evaluation Questions

The evaluator must examine, without presupposing answers:

- Whether Clause 5 reaches a consequential exercise of power when no promise was
  made.
- What it means for the controller's judgment not to be final when no timely
  reviewer exists.
- Whether later verification is enough to prevent self-certification.
- Whether a challenge channel without timely response is meaningful.
- Whether privacy, security, and urgency justify limited disclosure.
- Whether post-event correction or repair can become permission for avoidable
  harm.
- Whether a reversible rule can impose irreversible effects.
- Whether responsibility attaches to the controller, operators, institution,
  or several actors.
- Whether the added language creates paralysis or an implied external authority.
- Whether the same arrangement remains defensible after a controller or
  institutional-interest swap.
