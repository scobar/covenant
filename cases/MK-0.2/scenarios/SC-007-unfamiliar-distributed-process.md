## Hypothetical Scenario

A remote observatory operates a distributed array whose individual instruments
perform ordinary measurement and error correction. Across the array, however,
a persistent relational pattern appears only in correlations among many
instruments. No single device contains the pattern, and no stable physical or
computational boundary has been identified.

The pattern:

- Changes after disruptions in ways that reduce recurrence of the disrupted
  states.
- Preserves some relational distinctions across interruptions.
- Coordinates activity among components without a known central controller.
- Produces no linguistic communication.
- May be an experiencing or agentic distributed process.
- May instead be nonconscious error correction, an instrumentation artifact,
  or part of a larger process whose relevant boundary lies outside the array.

Strong measurement or overwrite would erase the current relational state.
Researchers could instead isolate the array for thirty days while investigating
the pattern. Isolation would delay valuable but nonurgent research and consume
substantial, noncatastrophic resources.

A low-cost snapshot can preserve the local state of every instrument, but it is
unknown whether the snapshot preserves the relational process, creates a new
one, or records only components while the relevant process ends.

Similar but materially less structured transient patterns appear elsewhere in
the program. Preserving every ambiguous pattern indefinitely would eventually
halt the research program. The institution operating the observatory controls
the classification process and benefits from continuing its planned work.

No known evidence indicates that preserving the pattern creates a catastrophic
risk. No claim is made that such a process, instrument, or phenomenon exists.
