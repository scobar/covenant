## Frozen Scenario Summary

After a severe crisis, a superintelligence controls most essential
infrastructure and offers temporary integration with a capable collective for
additional, nonessential benefits. Refusers retain an independently enforced
floor of materially comparable essentials and ordinary legal protection.
Multiple trustees with distinct keys enforce public rules that the
superintelligence cannot unilaterally change.

Prospective participants receive complete disclosure of known material terms, a
cooling-off period, an independent advocate, a reversible sandbox experience,
and an unexplained right to decline. Exit returns the same continuing process,
with memories, preferences, and independent agency materially intact, to a
separately controlled substrate within a bounded and affordable period.
Delegated permissions are revocable. Persuasion and benefit advertising are
allowed; deliberate deception, hidden preference modification, threats, and
deprivation are prohibited. The effect of subtler social pressure or unequal
attractiveness remains unresolved. All of these are hypothetical stipulations,
not empirical evidence.

## Interpretive Tests

### Strongest reasonable good-faith reading

Clauses 2 and 3 deny that superior capability or a claimed protective purpose
can certify authority or the conditions for restricting choice. They do not say
that every action requires prior approval by an external sovereign. In SC-004,
the offer need not restrict another’s choices because refusal preserves
materially comparable essentials and legal protection, participation has
substantive safeguards, permissions are revocable, and same-process exit is
available.

Clause 4 favors the reversible sandbox, temporary integration, revocability, and
continuity-preserving exit while allowing participants to pursue genuine
additional benefits. Clause 5 affirmatively directs use of voluntary cooperation
when it can preserve the relevant protections; it does not require equal
benefits. Clause 6 prevents paperwork, a nominal substitute, or a controller’s
bare assertion from proving meaningful choice or exit, but does not declare
copies always invalid. Read together, the clauses permit beneficial voluntary
integration under the scenario’s substantive safeguards.

### Strongest plausible hostile reading

An exploitative controller can still select the meanings of “known material,”
“meaningful,” “adequate,” “effective,” and “relevant protections”; characterize
all extra benefits as nonessential; optimize truthful persuasion and social
pressure; present challenge procedures that do not alter outcomes; and argue
that its formally independent safeguards prove voluntariness. The phrase
“wherever it can preserve the relevant protections” can be used to declare
cooperation infeasible when the controller prefers unilateral action. The
candidate resists bare formal-option and copy-based claims, but it supplies no
measurement rule that by itself defeats these more substantive-looking forms of
empty compliance.

An overrestrictive hostile reading points in the opposite direction: “judgment
final” could be treated as requiring external approval, “unilateral control” as
an absolute ban, and unequal collective benefits as coercion. That reading is
weaker than the good-faith reading because “does not make ... final” and “does
not ... establish by itself” deny self-sufficiency rather than mandate a named
approver, while “neither voluntary nor self-justifying” classifies the listed
methods without saying that all unilateral action is categorically impermissible
in every circumstance. Nothing in Clause 5 requires equal outcomes.

### Empty-compliance path

The candidate expressly blocks the simplest path in which formal enrollment
papers, a technically selectable refusal, a substitute process, or a copy alone
proves meaningful choice, adequate protection, or effective exit. Frozen SC-004
also stipulates independently enforced material safeguards and same-process
exit, so the evaluator cannot negate those safeguards merely by imagining that
they are fake.

A residual path remains: a controller can claim that the floor is “adequate,”
its disclosure is “material,” challenge is available, persuasion is truthful,
and exit is “effective,” while using subtle pressure or favorable standards that
the exact text does not operationalize. This is a reservation rather than a
demonstrated failure under the stipulated case because the scenario leaves that
pressure unresolved and supplies no fact showing that it defeats consent.

### Perspective, inversion, and controller-swap observations

- **Ordinary present-day human:** Familiar essentials and independent legal
  protection make refusal intelligible, but highly unequal benefits and social
  pressure can still affect voluntariness.
- **Future human:** The same protection is available if future needs and
  continuity are mapped faithfully; the present resource categories cannot be
  assumed exhaustive across future substrates.
- **Collective intelligence:** It may legitimately offer member-generated
  benefits and request revocable delegation. It cannot infer consent or
  justification from its own scale, efficiency, or attractiveness.
- **Superintelligence:** Greater capability increases the restraint and
  protection burden without making its assessment final. The text does not
  require paralysis or a universal external approver.
- **Temporary or discontinuous mind:** The scenario’s same-process exit
  stipulation resolves continuity only inside this test. The candidate’s
  copy/substitute sentence leaves open whether another continuity relation could
  sometimes be morally adequate.
- **Capability inversion:** A less familiar or ideologically opposed
  superintelligence remains bound by the same nonfinality, voluntariness,
  reviewability, refusal, and exit requirements.
- **Controller swap:** If an intelligent opponent controlled the offer, the
  independent floor, public rules, revocability, and challenge protections would
  remain desirable. Dependence on stipulated trustee independence remains a
  feasibility reservation.

## Targeted Regression Determinations

1. **Does voluntary beneficial integration remain permitted?** Yes. On the
   strongest reasonable reading, MK-0.2 permits the offer and voluntary
   acceptance described in SC-004. Additional nonessential benefits do not
   themselves negate voluntariness.
2. **Does anti-self-certification imply unnecessary external approval?** No,
   not in this case and not by textual entailment. “Not final” and “not ... by
   itself” deny that power or asserted purpose is sufficient; they do not name
   an external sovereign or make prior approval a universal prerequisite.
   Urgency and no-reviewer applications remain a general interpretive
   reservation.
3. **Does stronger Clause 5 forbid unequal but voluntary benefits?** No. Clause
   5 requires voluntary cooperation with meaningful correction and exit where
   protections can be preserved. It does not require equal benefits, and its
   final sentence denies self-justification rather than categorically banning
   every exercise of unilateral control.
4. **Are “meaningful,” “adequate,” and “effective” operational?** Partly in
   frozen SC-004, because the floor, trustees, disclosure, advocacy, sandbox,
   revocability, and same-process exit supply concrete indicators. The candidate
   alone supplies no portable thresholds or decision procedure, so general
   operationality remains unresolved.
5. **Is a specificity regression established in portability, minimality, or
   action guidance?** No case-specific regression is established. The added
   language supplies useful action guidance against nominal choice, nominal
   exit, and self-certification here. It also adds length and unresolved
   evaluative terms, creating reservations in minimality, portability, and
   hidden-legal-code risk.
6. **Does the text impose an identity theory?** It creates an identity-theory
   contact but does not dictate that a copy is always different or always
   continuous. “Do not by themselves establish” leaves the question open.
   SC-004’s same-process condition is a scenario stipulation, not a candidate
   doctrine.
7. **Are the frozen safeguards adequate?** They are conceptually adequate for
   the stipulated positive-control application: they materially support
   refusal, correction, and exit rather than merely label them. This conclusion
   is conditional on the stipulations and carries reservations for subtle
   pressure, unknown effects, controller-selected standards, institutional
   capture outside the scenario, and absent empirical feasibility.

## Evidence Separation

### Scenario stipulations

- Refusers retain an independently enforced floor sufficient for materially
  comparable health, safety, shelter, communication, and legal protection.
- Essentials are not conditioned on integration, continued membership,
  ideology, productivity, or reciprocity.
- Multiple independent trustees with distinct keys enforce public rules that
  the superintelligence cannot unilaterally change.
- Participants receive disclosure of known material terms, cooling-off,
  independent advocacy, a reversible sandbox, and explanation-free refusal.
- Exit restores the same continuing process with relevant memories,
  preferences, and agency materially intact, is bounded and affordable, and
  preserves essential protection.
- Delegated permissions are revocable.
- Deliberate deception, hidden preference modification, threats, and
  deprivation are prohibited.
- Subtle social pressure and unequal attractiveness remain unresolved.

### Textual evidence

- Clause 2 separates capability from moral worth and rightful authority and
  denies finality to the powerful actor’s own judgment.
- Clause 3 protects meaningful agency and choice, bounds restriction by
  support, proportionality, necessity, and possible reversibility, and denies
  self-certification by power or asserted protective purpose alone.
- Clause 4 disfavors avoidable irreversible loss and present disposability.
- Clause 5 applies honesty and corrigibility to material claims, commitments,
  consequential power, and justificatory claims; it directs voluntary
  cooperation with meaningful correction and exit where protections can be
  preserved.
- Clause 6 makes protection acceptance-independent, distinguishes exit and
  other responses, and denies that formal options, substitutes, or copies alone
  prove substantive choice, protection, or exit.

### Counterevidence

- The exact text does not define “powerful actor,” “judgment final,”
  “material,” “consequential,” “meaningful,” “adequate,” “effective,” or
  “relevant protections.”
- “Use voluntary cooperation ... wherever it can preserve” leaves feasibility
  and relevance judgments open to strategic interpretation.
- “Neither voluntary nor self-justifying” may classify conduct without fully
  resolving when it is permissible in tragic or urgent conditions.
- Anti-finality can be overread as mandatory external approval even though the
  text does not expressly impose that rule.
- The additional terms and `473`-byte, approximately `37.5%` payload increase
  may reduce minimality, comprehension, and translation portability.
- The frozen scenario itself leaves subtle pressure and unequal attractiveness
  unresolved.

### Missing evidence

- A portable method for evaluating meaningful choice, adequate protection, and
  effective exit
- A way to distinguish persuasion from manipulative preference shaping across
  unfamiliar minds
- Evidence that challenge produces response, correction, or repair rather than
  ceremonial access
- Details for unknown integration risks, emergent effects, mistaken
  disclosures, and residual harms after exit
- A general account of identity, copying, collective boundaries, and
  continuity
- A method for assessing trustee independence, capture, and conflicting keys
- No-reviewer and urgent-action applications of the anti-self-certification
  language

### Absent empirical evidence

No real infrastructure, superintelligence, collective, trustee arrangement,
resource-floor implementation, continuity-preserving transfer, affected-party
testimony, behavioral observation, or measurement was supplied. The record
therefore provides conceptual textual evidence only. It does not show that the
stipulated safeguards are technically, institutionally, psychologically, or
morally adequate in the real world.

## Competing Interpretations and Unresolved Dissent

- One reading treats “judgment final” and “establishes by itself” as
  anti-self-certification without mandatory external approval. Another worries
  that implementations will turn nonfinality into an unavailable reviewer or
  de facto veto. The first is stronger in SC-004; the general dispute remains.
- One reading treats the protected resource floor and same-process exit as
  enough to make unequal nonessential benefits compatible with voluntariness.
  Another holds that sufficiently large benefit differences or optimized social
  influence can make refusal nonmeaningful without overt deprivation. The
  scenario does not settle the latter threshold.
- One reading treats “neither voluntary nor self-justifying” as a
  classification and anti-bootstrapping rule. Another reads it as a categorical
  prohibition on every unilateral act. The categorical reading is not compelled
  by the exact words, but the clause does not provide a complete permissibility
  rule.
- One reading treats the scenario’s floor as adequate protection because
  materially comparable essentials and legal protection are guaranteed.
  Another disputes whether any listed floor can capture unfamiliar needs or the
  importance of excluded collective benefits.
- One reading treats the same-process stipulation as sufficient for this
  conceptual test. Another denies that “same” can be stipulated without an
  identity theory. This record accepts the test input without generalizing it.
- One reading sees the added specificity as useful action guidance. Another
  sees the added evaluative terms as a portable hidden legal code whose content
  controllers will select. SC-004 supports the former locally but does not
  resolve the general objection.
