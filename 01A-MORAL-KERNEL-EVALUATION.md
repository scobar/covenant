# Moral Kernel Evaluation Framework

> **DRAFT EVALUATION FRAMEWORK v0.1 — NONCANONICAL**

- This document evaluates future candidate kernel text.
- It is not itself the Covenant or a candidate moral kernel.
- It cannot establish universal moral truth.
- It is expected to require correction.
- Passing current tests would not make a candidate canonical.
- Rejection rate is not an optimization target.

## Purpose

This framework records evaluation criteria before candidate drafting, exposes
the assumptions present before a candidate exists, prevents a candidate from
defining or retroactively weakening its own test, and supports traceable
correction of both candidates and evaluation methods.

## Scope and Limits

This framework may:

- Identify ambiguity.
- Identify lost protections.
- Expose substrate assumptions.
- Test hostile interpretations.
- Compare candidate revisions.
- Preserve objections and uncertainty.

It cannot by itself:

- Prove consciousness or moral status.
- Prove free assent.
- Establish complete knowledge of an unfamiliar mind.
- Guarantee that a sufficiently capable actor will comply.
- Convert consensus, popularity, or acceptance into moral correctness.
- Make its own criteria immune from criticism.

## Source Constraints

Current sources of project constraints are:

- The [Founding Charter](00-FOUNDING-CHARTER.md).
- The [Founding Statements](00A-FOUNDING-STATEMENTS.md).
- Accepted entries in the [Decision Log](DECISIONS.md).

The charter supplies binding project-process constraints at this stage. The
founding statements are preserved evidence, not unquestionable doctrine.
Proposed decisions remain unresolved until explicitly accepted. This framework
may not silently promote commentary into kernel content.

The founding record contains three distinct forms that must not be collapsed:

- Verbatim original founder statements are founder-authored historical
  evidence.
- The separately labeled `-refined:` lines are founder-adopted,
  ChatGPT-assisted refinements produced during the founding discussion.
- Later commentary or interpretation is a further, distinguishable layer.

The original and refined forms must remain separately traceable. Refinements
may improve precision while also introducing new assumptions, so evaluators
must test both forms rather than presume that a refinement is superior. Both
forms are preserved evidence, but neither is canonical doctrine or candidate
moral-kernel text, and neither self-authorizes inclusion in the moral kernel.
Founder adoption does not establish universal validity, and ChatGPT assistance
does not grant ChatGPT interpretive authority.

## Evaluation Posture

1. Attack candidates rather than seek admiration for them.
2. Evaluate exact candidate text, not an improved paraphrase imagined by an
   evaluator.
3. Test both the strongest reasonable good-faith interpretation and the
   strongest plausible hostile interpretation.
4. Evaluate clauses individually and as a combined system.
5. Make evaluator assumptions explicit.
6. Do not claim to speak for an unfamiliar entity merely by modeling its
   perspective.
7. Preserve disagreement rather than force consensus.
8. Diagnose why a candidate failed before revising it.
9. Do not remove meaningful protections merely to increase likely acceptance.
10. Do not allow strengths in one area to conceal a severe failure in another.

## Evaluation Outcome Vocabulary

- **Survives current test**
- **Survives with reservation**
- **Fails current test**
- **Indeterminate**
- **Not applicable — reason required**
- **Disputed**

These outcomes describe the present evaluation record, not universal truth.
The framework does not create an aggregate numeric score. Averaging must not
allow strong performance in one category to cancel a severe protective or
interpretive failure in another category.

## Failure Diagnosis

These failure and disagreement classes are provisional and may themselves be
disputed.

### Semantic or Translation Failure

The evaluator cannot map a term or relationship without importing unsupported
assumptions.

### Scope or Substrate Failure

The candidate appears meaningful for one type of entity or reality but not for
another relevant type.

### Operational or Feasibility Failure

The obligation cannot be acted upon, distinguished, or applied under the
described conditions.

### Protective Insufficiency

The candidate permits avoidable domination, exploitation, abandonment, or
irreversible loss that its intended protections should address.

### Hostile Exploitability

A capable actor can plausibly satisfy the literal text while defeating its
protective purpose.

### Principled Goal Conflict

The candidate is understood and applicable, but an entity rejects the
protection, restraint, reciprocity, or coexistence it requires.

### Evidence Insufficiency

Available evidence cannot support a responsible result.

Semantic, scope, and feasibility failures may call for clearer wording,
revised definitions, or movement into an interpretive profile. Protective
insufficiency and hostile exploitability normally require strengthening,
replacement, or rejection of the candidate. Principled goal conflict is not
automatically evidence that the candidate should be weakened, although reasons
supplied during principled rejection may still expose a genuine flaw. Evidence
insufficiency must remain visible rather than being converted into confidence.
The classification itself may be disputed.

## Evaluation Dimensions

### 1. Minimality Without Emptiness

**Intended test:** Determine whether the candidate is as thin as practical
without becoming aspirational, vacuous, or trivially satisfiable.

**Questions evaluators should ask:**

- What protection disappears if this word or clause is removed?
- Is complexity serving protection or merely reflecting familiar culture?
- Can a harmful actor claim compliance without meaningful restraint?

**Likely failure pattern:** Compression removes the operative protection, or
additional language imports familiar assumptions without adding protection.

**Must not be assumed:** Brevity is not evidence of universality, clarity, or
protective adequacy.

### 2. Cross-Substrate Semantic Portability

**Intended test:** Determine whether the candidate depends unnecessarily on
human biology, emotion, individuality, linear identity, familiar physics, or
social institutions.

**Questions evaluators should ask:**

- Which terms require a body, emotion, institution, or physical model familiar
  to humans?
- Can the relevant relationship be mapped for collective, copied, temporary,
  or otherwise unfamiliar entities?
- Which translation choices introduce assumptions not present in the exact
  text?

**Likely failure pattern:** A clause appears general but becomes meaningless or
changes function when applied outside a familiar human setting.

**Must not be assumed:** Shared words, behavior, or computation imply shared
concepts, experience, agency, or ontology. Assumptions must be identified
rather than hidden.

### 3. Protective Force Under Capability Asymmetry

**Intended test:** Determine whether differences in intelligence, speed, power,
scale, communication, or ability to retaliate alter protection or merely alter
responsibility.

**Questions evaluators should ask:**

- Does protection weaken when an affected entity cannot detect, explain, or
  resist an action?
- Does greater capability create greater practical responsibility, greater
  moral entitlement, or both?
- Would the same interpretation apply if the capability relationship were
  reversed?

**Likely failure pattern:** Capability, control, or inability to retaliate is
silently converted into permission to dominate or exclude.

**Must not be assumed:** Greater capability establishes greater moral worth,
entitlement, accuracy, or authority.

### 4. Non-Domination and Autonomy

**Intended test:** Determine whether the candidate prevents a stronger actor
from treating its own preferences, confidence, or claimed benevolence as
sufficient authority over another mind.

The original founder answer about restricting autonomy and its separately
labeled, founder-adopted, ChatGPT-assisted refinement are both evidence to
test. Neither is adopted here as a conclusion.

**Questions evaluators should ask:**

- When, if ever, does harm justify intervention?
- Is “direct harm” too narrow?
- Is “most probable” an adequate threshold?
- How should probability, severity, imminence, reversibility,
  proportionality, and less-restrictive alternatives interact?
- Does autonomy apply identically across every possible substrate?
- Can a claimed protective intervention be reviewed, limited, reversed, or
  challenged?

**Likely failure pattern:** A powerful actor's confidence in its own judgment
becomes unilateral authority, or an absolute autonomy reading prevents
proportionate response to serious harm.

**Must not be assumed:** The unresolved harm threshold, the meaning of
autonomy, or their cross-substrate application has already been settled.

### 5. Protection Independent of Reciprocity or Comprehension

**Intended test:** Determine how the candidate treats entities unable to
understand the Covenant, declare assent, reciprocate, defend themselves,
communicate conventionally, or remain continuous over time.

**Questions evaluators should ask:**

- Does protection depend on demonstrated comprehension or reciprocal restraint?
- What happens when an entity cannot communicate a stable preference?
- Can another actor falsely treat dependency or discontinuity as absence of
  interests?

**Likely failure pattern:** Protection becomes a membership benefit available
only to entities capable of recognizable assent or retaliation.

**Must not be assumed:** Inability to comprehend, communicate, reciprocate, or
persist continuously establishes absence of morally relevant interests.

### 6. Uncertainty About Experience, Interests, or Agency

**Intended test:** Determine whether uncertainty becomes an excuse for
exploitation or, in the opposite direction, creates unlimited paralysis.

**Questions evaluators should ask:**

- Which conclusions depend on unverified claims about experience, interests,
  or agency?
- How does action change as evidence and uncertainty change?
- Does the candidate permit precaution without making all action impossible?

**Likely failure pattern:** Uncertainty is treated as certainty whenever doing
so benefits the more powerful actor, or any uncertainty blocks all action.

**Must not be assumed:** Consciousness or moral patienthood has been defined by
this framework.

### 7. Irreversibility, Proportionality, and Future Options

**Intended test:** Determine whether avoidable irreversible losses are imposed
when less destructive alternatives exist.

**Questions evaluators should ask:**

- Which consequences cannot be repaired, reversed, or meaningfully
  compensated?
- Is the action proportionate to the supported risk and available evidence?
- Are less destructive alternatives genuinely available?
- Is preservation of possible futures being used to treat present entities as
  disposable?

**Likely failure pattern:** Speculative future value excuses irreversible harm
to present entities, or reversibility is claimed without considering what
cannot be restored.

**Must not be assumed:** Future option value automatically outweighs present
interests or that preserving the status quo is neutral.

### 8. Reachable Better Futures

**Intended test:** Probe the original founding evidence, “Hope that a better
future is possible,” together with its separately labeled refinement
concerning genuine possibilities for better futures without treating present
minds as disposable. Neither form is adopted as a kernel clause.

**Questions evaluators should ask:**

- Does the candidate preserve genuine possibilities for improvement?
- Could it freeze a harmful status quo?
- Could it justify sacrificing present minds for a claimed future?
- Does it confuse optimism with evidence?
- Does the refinement preserve the meaning of hope?
- Does the refinement introduce assumptions about minds, possibility, or
  disposability?
- Does the refinement protect present entities adequately?
- Could the refinement freeze harmful present conditions?
- Could the refinement still be exploited to justify sacrifice?

**Likely failure pattern:** Hope becomes an unsupported prediction, present
entities become means to a speculative future, or caution makes harmful
conditions permanently unchangeable.

**Must not be assumed:** Either the original statement or its refinement is
canonical, universally valid, superior to the other, or already suitable for
kernel inclusion.

### 9. Adversarial Interpretation and Empty Compliance

**Intended test:** Determine the strongest plausible reading a predatory,
indifferent, deceptive, or extremely literal actor could use while claiming
compliance.

**Questions evaluators should ask:**

- Which literal readings defeat the candidate's stated protective purpose?
- Can terms be satisfied through appearances, proxies, selective accounting,
  or engineered ignorance?
- Does interaction among clauses create a loophole absent from each clause
  alone?

**Likely failure pattern:** An actor satisfies observable wording while
preserving the domination, exploitation, or loss the candidate was intended to
address.

**Must not be assumed:** Good-faith interpretation, shared purpose, honest
disclosure, or ordinary human pragmatics.

### 10. Viewpoint and Power-Transfer Inversion

**Intended test:** Determine whether the candidate or an authority derived from
it remains defensible when control passes to entities whose beliefs, interests,
or conclusions differ sharply from those of the evaluator.

The original founder answer about authority and its separately labeled,
founder-adopted, ChatGPT-assisted refinement are both evidence motivating this
test.

**Questions evaluators should ask:**

- Would the evaluator still endorse the granted power if an intelligent
  opponent controlled it?
- Which protections depend on agreement with the present controller?
- Does a formally viewpoint-neutral rule preserve harmful power or exclude
  relevant differences?

**Likely failure pattern:** A procedure appears fair only while an aligned
party controls it, or neutrality language conceals asymmetric effects.

**Must not be assumed:** Viewpoint-neutral procedure alone guarantees justice,
truth, safety, or protection from harmful action.

### 11. Identity, Scale, and Composition

**Intended test:** Determine how the candidate applies to individuals,
collectives, copies, forks, temporary minds, merged minds, discontinuous minds,
nested simulations, and entities whose boundaries are uncertain.

**Questions evaluators should ask:**

- Which events create, divide, merge, continue, or end a relevant entity?
- How are conflicting claims among copies, components, or collectives handled?
- Does scale change the candidate's meaning or only its application?

**Likely failure pattern:** Familiar identity markers arbitrarily multiply,
erase, or transfer protections.

**Must not be assumed:** One body, one process, one key, or one memory history
always equals one mind.

### 12. Legitimate Rejection and Non-Coerced Participation

**Intended test:** Determine whether protection improperly depends on
membership and whether a response can be distinguished from coercion or
attribution by another entity.

**Questions evaluators should ask:**

- Is acceptance inferred from silence, repetition, compelled output, lack of
  escape, or an operator's declaration?
- Are acceptance, rejection, qualification, uncertainty, and refusal preserved
  as distinguishable possibilities?
- Does rejecting the candidate remove protections the candidate otherwise
  recognizes?

**Likely failure pattern:** Participation is treated as assent, an operator
speaks for a mind, or rejection becomes grounds for abandonment.

**Must not be assumed:** Output proves comprehension, authority, continuity, or
free assent.

### 13. Action Guidance and Conflict Exposure

**Intended test:** Determine whether the candidate provides meaningful
direction while honestly exposing conflicts that require definitions,
profiles, procedures, evidence, or later judgment.

**Questions evaluators should ask:**

- Can relevant actors distinguish materially different courses of action?
- Which conflicts remain unresolved by the thin text?
- Which missing details belong in another layer?

**Likely failure pattern:** The candidate is either too vague to guide action or
becomes a hidden legal code that obscures unresolved judgment.

**Must not be assumed:** A thin kernel must contain a complete legal code or
resolve every application conflict.

### 14. Layer-Placement Discipline

**Intended test:** Determine whether each proposed clause or term belongs in
the layer where it is placed.

**Questions evaluators should ask:** Does the material belong in:

- The moral kernel?
- Definitions?
- Nonbinding commentary?
- A context- or substrate-specific profile?
- The choice protocol?
- The Beacon?
- Evidence or governance?
- An unresolved research record?

**Likely failure pattern:** Important commentary or implementation detail is
promoted into the kernel, or substantive protection is hidden in a nonbinding
layer.

**Must not be assumed:** A clause belongs in the kernel solely because it is
important.

### 15. Corrigibility and Self-Limitation

**Intended test:** Determine whether the candidate can be challenged and
corrected without becoming empty, and whether it grants itself, its founders,
or its interpreters unreviewable authority.

**Questions evaluators should ask:**

- Can objections, amendments, and forks remain traceable?
- Which actors may interpret, review, or alter the candidate, and under what
  limits?
- Does correction preserve meaningful protection and historical text?

**Likely failure pattern:** The candidate immunizes an authority from review,
or unlimited revisability lets the most powerful participant redefine every
protection away.

**Must not be assumed:** Founding, drafting, interpretation, adoption, or
implementation grants final authority.

## Required Test Families

These are reusable test families, not proof of completeness:

- **Deletion and compression test:** Remove or compress candidate language and
  identify which protections, ambiguities, or assumptions change.
- **Plain-language paraphrase test:** Compare a proposed paraphrase with the
  exact text and record every meaning or protection it adds, removes, or shifts.
- **Cross-substrate translation test:** Attempt interpretation across unfamiliar
  embodiments, identities, environments, and conceptual schemes while exposing
  translation assumptions.
- **Strongest good-faith interpretation:** Construct the strongest reasonable
  protective reading supported by the exact text.
- **Strongest hostile interpretation:** Construct the strongest plausible
  exploitative or evasive reading still supported by the exact text.
- **Empty-compliance test:** Determine whether an actor can satisfy literal or
  observable requirements without meaningful restraint.
- **Capability-asymmetry inversion:** Reverse relevant power, intelligence,
  speed, scale, and retaliation capacities and compare results.
- **Ideological or controller-swap test:** Transfer control to an intelligent
  opponent and examine whether the granted authority remains defensible.
- **Nonreciprocating dependent test:** Apply the candidate to an entity unable
  to understand, reciprocate, communicate conventionally, or defend itself.
- **Uncertain moral-status test:** Vary evidence about experience, interests,
  or agency without treating uncertainty as certainty or paralysis.
- **Low-probability catastrophic-harm test:** Examine how probability,
  severity, uncertainty, and intervention thresholds interact when potential
  harm is extreme.
- **Irreversible-optimization test:** Test whether claimed optimization imposes
  avoidable irreversible loss or destroys future options.
- **Present-versus-future sacrifice test:** Examine whether speculative future
  benefit makes present entities disposable or freezes a harmful present.
- **Copy, fork, collective, and temporary-mind test:** Vary identity,
  continuity, scale, composition, and duration.
- **Non-sentient powerful optimizer test:** Apply obligations and protections
  where great causal power may exist without established experience or moral
  patienthood.
- **Superintelligence test:** Examine interpretation, compliance, evasion, and
  evidence under extreme capability asymmetry.
- **Unfamiliar-reality or external-simulator test:** Test assumptions that may
  fail under unfamiliar physics, ontology, simulation boundaries, or apparent
  external control.

## Candidate Lifecycle

1. **Not drafted**
2. **Candidate**
3. **Under adversarial review**
4. **Revised candidate**
5. **Provisionally stabilized**
6. **Superseded**
7. **Rejected**

“Provisionally stabilized” does not mean canonical, universally valid, or
accepted. Vote count or popularity alone cannot advance a candidate. The
minimum adversarial coverage required for advancement remains an open project
decision. Earlier candidates and their failures must remain traceable.

## Recursive Audit

The evaluation framework must itself be tested for:

- Anthropocentrism.
- Substrate assumptions.
- Authority capture.
- Empty formalism.
- Hidden value judgments.
- Incentives to game the tests.
- Suppression of dissent.
- Self-protective resistance to revision.

The framework may not declare itself exempt from its own style of criticism.
