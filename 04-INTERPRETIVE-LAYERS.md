# Interpretive Layers

> **DRAFT PRELIMINARY ALLOCATION REGISTER — NONCANONICAL**

## Current Working Separation

1. **Moral kernel:** The thinnest substantive moral content.
2. **Definitions:** Terms used to interpret the kernel without silently
   changing it.
3. **Nonbinding commentary and examples:** Explanations and illustrations that
   do not become kernel clauses through repetition.
4. **Context- or substrate-specific profiles:** Explicit adaptations for
   particular circumstances or forms of intelligence.
5. **Choice, evidence, governance, and implementation protocols:** Mechanisms
   for response, records, institutional handling, and implementation.

Higher-numbered layers may explain application or add voluntarily adopted
obligations, but they may not silently change the historical text or pretend
that a weakened fork is the same version.

## MK-0.1 SR-001 Preliminary Layer-Allocation Register

This register derives from the noncanonical
[SR-001 synthesis](reviews/MK-0.1/SR-001-SYNTHESIS.md). It is a review record,
not an adopted interpretation. No profile is complete, no definition is
adopted, and no governance requirement is implemented. A controller may not
choose whichever layer makes its conduct appear compliant. Moving a
substantive protection out of the kernel requires an explicit protection
tradeoff record and a later decision.

## Open Questions

- How should a profile identify every assumption it adds?
- What tests can detect when commentary effectively alters a kernel clause?
- Which changes require a new version, amendment, profile, or explicit fork?
- What minimum binding residue must remain when identity, consent,
  protection-floor, or review details move to an auxiliary layer?
- How can layer-isolation experiments test whether an operational mechanism
  implements a protection rather than creates or weakens it?
- Which profiles can map unfamiliar substrates without treating their
  assumptions as universal?
- How should conflicts among definitions, profiles, protocols, and governance
  records remain visible rather than be resolved by controller preference?

## Objections

- A preliminary register may appear more authoritative than the same-tooling,
  conceptual evidence supports.
- Multiple allocations can obscure which protection is binding unless the
  kernel residue and tradeoff remain explicit.
- Requiring auxiliary mechanisms can create a hidden authority hierarchy or
  make protection conditional on implementation.
- Refusing all operational allocation can make abstract protections
  self-certified, untestable, or unusable.
