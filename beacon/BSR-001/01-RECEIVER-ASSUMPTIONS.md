# BSR-001 — Receiver Assumptions

> **DRAFT BEACON BOOTSTRAP RESEARCH v0.1 — NONCANONICAL — NO SIGNAL ATTEMPT OR DECODING TRIAL EXECUTED**

## Research-only boundary

BSR-001 is an evaluation and research framework, not a signal or final Beacon
specification. It provides no evidence that an unknown intelligence can decode
a message or would accept the Covenant. It does not choose a moral-kernel
candidate or authorize `SIG-001`, a model run, a decoding trial, a holdout,
transmission, or distribution.

No receiver, decoder, model, event stream, signal, carrier, payload, test, or
result is selected or created here. These assumptions may require substantial
correction. BSR-001 can advance while PX-001 remains dormant, but final Beacon
payload optimization must still wait for sufficient moral-payload
stabilization.

Every future signal attempt must identify the assumptions it requires, the
layer or operation that requires each assumption, and which assumptions remain
untested. Recording an assumption does not establish that it is universal,
necessary, sufficient, true of an unknown receiver, or satisfied by any
particular system.

## Assumption groups

### Minimal observational assumptions

Candidate assumptions to examine include:

- ability to distinguish at least two observable event or state classes;
- ability to observe some form of order, adjacency, recurrence, or relation;
- ability to preserve state or memory long enough to compare observations;
- ability to detect repetition and difference;
- ability to count finite recurrences or construct an equivalent relation;
- ability to form hypotheses and test predictions;
- sufficient resources to investigate structured regularity; and
- ability to separate some signal behavior from background behavior.

These are research candidates. BSR-001 does not call them universally
necessary or sufficient, require that they occur together, or assume that a
receiver realizes them in a human-familiar way. Later alternatives should
attempt to remove or weaken each assumption and record the cost of doing so.

### Assumptions not permitted by default

A future attempt must not assume by default:

- human language;
- human mathematical notation;
- Arabic numerals;
- binary notation;
- left-to-right reading;
- a raster image;
- a specific sensory modality;
- sound;
- vision;
- human biology;
- human emotion;
- one body per mind;
- stable individual identity;
- linear subjective time;
- familiar social institutions;
- familiar physics;
- familiar moral concepts;
- benevolence;
- reciprocity;
- curiosity;
- a desire to cooperate; or
- a desire to respond.

If a later design requires any such assumption, it must identify and justify
the assumption explicitly rather than smuggling it into framing, examples,
scoring, or interpretation.

### Capability thresholds

Research and later trials must distinguish:

- detection capability;
- framing capability;
- mathematical inference;
- grammar induction;
- program or model execution;
- physical-model reconstruction;
- semantic-model induction;
- moral and agency interpretation; and
- response construction.

These capabilities must not be merged into one intelligence threshold. A
receiver may exhibit any subset, may reach them through unfamiliar methods,
or may fail a nominally earlier capability while finding an alternative path
to a later artifact. Any claimed alternative path would require explicit
evidence and must not silently erase the failed requirement.

### Receiver families to test eventually

Future research categories include:

- present human decoder;
- present AI model;
- future AI model;
- collective intelligence;
- distributed process;
- non-sentient but highly capable optimizer;
- entity with unfamiliar embodiment;
- entity with unfamiliar physics; and
- entity apparently outside our universe or simulation.

These are categories for later test design, not selected decoders or claims to
represent any category. No person, model, provider, collective, process, or
entity is identified, contacted, assigned, simulated, or tested here.

### Motivation and adversarial behavior

A receiver may:

- ignore the signal;
- misinterpret it;
- deliberately return a false decode;
- game a known scorecard;
- infer the solution through contamination;
- understand the signal but reject its contents; or
- use the message strategically.

Consequently, observed output would not automatically establish comprehension,
good faith, agency, acceptance, or the receiver's own choice. Motivation,
capability, reconstruction, recognition, retrieval, strategic behavior, and
assent require separate treatment.
