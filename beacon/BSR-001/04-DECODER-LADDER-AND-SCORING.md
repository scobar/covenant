# BSR-001 — Decoder Ladder and Scoring Framework

> **DRAFT BEACON BOOTSTRAP RESEARCH v0.1 — NONCANONICAL — NO SIGNAL ATTEMPT OR DECODING TRIAL EXECUTED**

## Research-Only Boundary

BSR-001 is an evaluation and research framework. It is not a signal or a final
Beacon specification, and it is not evidence that any unknown intelligence can
decode a message or would accept the Covenant. It does not choose a
moral-kernel candidate or authorize SIG-001, model execution, transmission, or
distribution. It may require substantial correction. BSR-001 can advance while
PX-001 remains dormant, but that does not weaken the requirement that
moral-payload stabilization precede final Beacon payload optimization.

No signal attempt, decoder, decoding trial, score, empirical result, or
probability exists in this framework.

## Diagnostic Purpose

The ladder is diagnostic. It is not:

- A universal intelligence score.
- A moral score.
- A candidate ranking.
- A probability that an unknown intelligence will decode a signal.
- Evidence of assent.

Levels distinguish kinds of recovery rather than defining a single intelligence
threshold. Partial or later-layer recovery must not hide an unresolved
lower-layer failure.

## Decoder Levels

### Level 0 — Artificiality

The decoder identifies structured behavior inconsistent with its best natural
or accidental explanation and states why artificial construction is a serious
hypothesis.

### Level 1 — Framing

The decoder recovers enough event, boundary, recurrence, ordering, grouping,
or synchronization rules to predict unseen structural continuations.

### Level 2 — Numeracy

The decoder recovers counting, succession, or an equivalent finite-quantity
relation and maps at least one compact representation to it.

### Level 3 — Mathematics

The decoder recovers arithmetic or other mathematical relations and correctly
predicts withheld mathematical statements.

### Level 4 — Grammar and logic

The decoder recovers compositional syntax, truth or validity relations,
variables, rules, or logical transformations sufficient to interpret unseen
formal expressions.

### Level 5 — Physical reference

The decoder identifies the message’s physical-reference profile, distinguishes
dimensionless from dimensional anchors, and reconstructs at least one intended
reference relation.

### Level 6 — Shared executable models

The decoder correctly executes, simulates, or predicts state transitions in a
formal model taught by the message.

### Level 7 — Agency and consequence semantics

The decoder distinguishes modeled agents, choices, capability asymmetry,
constraint, harm, irreversibility, correction, cooperation, coercion, or exit
in withheld contrast cases.

### Level 8 — Covenant layers and provenance

The decoder distinguishes exact normative payload, definitions, commentary,
profiles, objections, uncertainty, versions, and provenance without treating
them as one undifferentiated command.

### Level 9 — Response

The decoder constructs a syntactically and semantically valid response that
can distinguish at least:

- Acceptance.
- Rejection.
- Qualified or scoped acceptance.
- Uncertainty.
- Request for clarification.
- Refusal to determine.

Level 9 remains design-only because no choice protocol or response syntax is
finalized.

## Per-Level Outcome Vocabulary

Use only:

- `Recovered`
- `Partially recovered`
- `Not recovered`
- `Indeterminate`
- `Contaminated`
- `Invalid trial`

Every future per-level record requires:

- Evidence.
- Counterevidence.
- Confidence.
- Alternative interpretation.
- Required artifact.
- Prediction or withheld test result.
- Scorer notes.

The vocabulary and required fields are preparation rules. They do not record
an outcome.

## Threshold Success

A trial may report success at a threshold only if all required artifacts
through that threshold are satisfied under the scorecard.

Do not infer that higher-level success cures a failed lower-level artifact
unless the scorecard explicitly documents an alternative path.

The threshold and its artifacts must be frozen before the corresponding trial
begins. No threshold has been selected or evaluated in BSR-001.

## Conditional Empirical Decode-Rate Reporting

The permitted future empirical report is:

`k successful valid trials out of n valid trials at or above Decoder Level L`

It may also be written as:

`k / n`

or as the conditional expression:

`P(recover at least Level L | exact signal attempt, decoder identity, context, tools, prompt, trial protocol)`

This is an empirical conditional rate, not a probability about all future
intelligences. The symbols above define a reporting form; they do not supply
values or assert an empirical result.

Every future empirical report must include:

- Requested trials.
- Completed trials.
- Valid trials.
- Invalid trials.
- Contaminated trials.
- Successes by level.
- Decoder/provider/model version.
- Context and tools.
- Prompt identity.
- Date.
- Small-sample limitations.
- Correlation and shared-model limitations.

Do not pool results across model versions, providers, or materially different
trial methods without an explicit later analysis.

## No Aggregate Universal Score

Do not create one weighted score combining all levels.

A highest recovered level may be reported descriptively, but the complete
per-level record must remain visible.

No finite trial set may be presented as universal decodability, a universal
probability, moral standing, assent, or authority.
