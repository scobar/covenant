# BSR-001 — Holdout and Training-Contamination Controls

> **DRAFT BEACON BOOTSTRAP RESEARCH v0.1 — NONCANONICAL — NO SIGNAL ATTEMPT OR DECODING TRIAL EXECUTED**

## Research-only boundary

This document defines future evidence controls only. It creates no public
attempt, sealed holdout, transformation, generator, mapping, solution, seed,
nonce, commitment, trial packet, decoder output, score, reveal, or publication
record.

BSR-001 is an evaluation and research framework. It is not a signal or a final
Beacon specification. It is not evidence that any unknown intelligence can
decode a message or that any entity would accept the Covenant. It does not
choose a moral-kernel candidate, authorize `SIG-001`, authorize model
execution, or authorize transmission or distribution. It may require
substantial correction.

BSR-001 can advance while PX-001 remains dormant. This work does not weaken the
requirement that moral-payload stabilization precede final Beacon payload
optimization.

## Public-attempt contamination

- The repository is public.
- Public signal attempts and their solutions may enter model training data,
  retrieval indexes, caches, benchmarks, conversations, or prior context.
- Later successful decoding of a public attempt may reflect reconstruction,
  memorization, recognition, retrieval, or a mixture.
- Public attempts remain valuable for transparency and debugging but are weak
  evidence against contamination.

Public provenance cannot prove what training data, retrieval state, memory, or
prior conversations influenced a future decoder.

## Two-track evidence design

Future evidence must distinguish:

1. **Public canonical attempts**
   - Exact committed attempt.
   - Public design and solution after publication.
   - Transparent history.
   - Useful for reproducibility and critique.
2. **Sealed holdout variants**
   - Surface-novel variants derived from a frozen formal design.
   - Signal, mapping, and solution withheld until output freezes.
   - Stored outside the public repository during active testing.
   - Published afterward only under later authorization.

The tracks answer different evidentiary questions and must not be silently
pooled. No public attempt or holdout exists now.

## Allowed future holdout transformations

Candidate transformations for later evaluation include:

- Symbol permutation.
- Event-class permutation.
- Timing or spacing-ratio transformation.
- Carrier-preserving scale transformation.
- Redundancy-seed variation.
- Checksum-instance variation.
- Example-set variation.
- Withheld continuation.
- Semantically equivalent formal-model instance.
- Reordered independent teaching examples where ordering is not semantic.

A transformation is valid only if it preserves the intended formal structure
being tested. Surface novelty alone does not establish formal equivalence,
independence, or absence of contamination. No transformation is selected, and
no generator is designed or implemented here.

## Holdout commitment

A future holdout procedure should record outside the repository:

- Attempt ID.
- Variant ID.
- Exact variant bytes or abstract events.
- Solution.
- Transformation rule.
- Random seed or nonce.
- Scorecard.
- SHA-256 commitment.
- Creation time.
- Custodian.
- Reveal conditions.

Only the commitment may be public before a trial if a later protocol
authorizes it. The signal, mapping, solution, seed or nonce, and complete
holdout record remain outside the public repository until the applicable
output freezes and later reveal is authorized.

A commitment may help detect post-hoc substitution of committed bytes. It does
not by itself prove correct generation, formal equivalence, fair
randomization, receiver ignorance, or absence of training contamination. No
holdout record, mapping, solution, seed, nonce, or commitment is created now.

## Contamination classes

Use these classes in a future trial record:

- `No known exposure`
- `Possible prior exposure`
- `Known public-attempt exposure`
- `Repository exposure`
- `Exact-text search exposure`
- `Solution exposure`
- `Training contamination unknown`
- `Contaminated trial`

`No known exposure` is not proof of no exposure. Classification must preserve
uncertainty rather than converting missing evidence into a clean-training
claim.

## Trial controls

A future trial must record:

- Public versus sealed attempt.
- Prior project exposure.
- Repository access.
- Web access.
- Exact-text searching.
- Memory.
- Retrieval.
- Operator knowledge.
- Whether the model identified the artifact from prior familiarity.

The record must also preserve any exposure discovered after output freezing.
Exposure changes the interpretation or classification of a trial; it must not
be silently removed to retain a favorable result.

## Post-trial reveal

A future sealed-track reveal must proceed in this order:

1. Freeze decoder output.
2. Freeze score.
3. Reveal mapping and solution.
4. Verify commitment.
5. Preserve deviations.
6. Publish or archive according to later authorization.

The reveal must not rewrite the frozen output, score, provenance, or
contamination record. A failed or discrepant commitment verification must
remain visible.

## No selective holdout publication

Do not publish only successful variants or suppress unexpected valid decodes.
Preserve failed, partial, indeterminate, contaminated, invalid, and unexpected
results under their applicable retention and authorization rules. Trial
selection and publication selection must be disclosed separately.

## Current nonexecution record

No actual public attempt, sealed variant, transformation, event stream,
mapping, solution, seed, nonce, commitment, trial, output, score, empirical
rate, reveal, archive, or publication exists in this document.
