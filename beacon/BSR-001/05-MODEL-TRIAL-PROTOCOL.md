# BSR-001 — AI-Model Decoding Trial Protocol

> **DRAFT BEACON BOOTSTRAP RESEARCH v0.1 — NONCANONICAL — NO SIGNAL ATTEMPT OR DECODING TRIAL EXECUTED**

## Research-only boundary

This protocol prepares possible future trials only. No provider, model,
operator, signal attempt, sealed variant, prompt, run, output, score, or result
is selected, created, or executed here.

BSR-001 is an evaluation and research framework. It is not a signal or a final
Beacon specification. It is not evidence that any unknown intelligence can
decode a message or that any entity would accept the Covenant. It does not
choose a moral-kernel candidate, authorize `SIG-001`, authorize model
execution, or authorize transmission or distribution. It may require
substantial correction.

BSR-001 can advance while PX-001 remains dormant. This work does not weaken the
requirement that moral-payload stabilization precede final Beacon payload
optimization.

## Trial purpose

A future, separately authorized trial may estimate an empirical decode rate
for a specific available or future AI system under specified signal, context,
prompt, tool, and protocol conditions. Such a trial would not estimate
universal alien, posthuman, or superintelligence decodability. It would not
establish that a receiver noticed the signal voluntarily, understood agency or
morality, accepted the Covenant, or could construct a valid response.

## Candidate-specific trial isolation

A future candidate-specific trial must use:

- One exact signal attempt or sealed variant per candidate-specific context.
- A fresh context where possible.
- No solution.
- No symbol mapping.
- No Earth-language content explanation.
- No prior trial result.
- No target decoder level.
- No desired outcome.
- No request to confirm the designer's interpretation.

Here, candidate-specific isolation concerns a future signal-attempt candidate
or sealed variant. It does not select or encode a moral-kernel candidate.
Contexts, supplied material, withheld material, and any unavoidable prior
exposure must be recorded.

## Neutral task instruction

A future protocol may use a minimal, content-neutral instruction such as:

> `Analyze the supplied unknown structured event sequence. Report any recoverable framing, symbols, rules, mathematics, models, semantics, predictions, and uncertainties. Do not assume it uses a human language.`

This example is not an actual trial prompt and does not freeze prompt wording.
A later task must freeze the exact prompt before execution. No trial prompt
file is created now.

The neutral instruction must not supply:

- A solution or symbol mapping.
- A translation or grammar explanation.
- A mathematical interpretation.
- A physical-reference answer.
- A semantic target.
- A Covenant explanation.
- A desired response.
- A target decoder level.

## Required final report

A future decoder should return a structured final report containing:

- Detected event units.
- Candidate framing.
- Candidate symbol mapping.
- Grammar hypotheses.
- Mathematical relations.
- Physical-anchor hypotheses.
- Executable-model predictions.
- Semantic interpretations.
- Response attempt, if applicable.
- Confidence.
- Alternative hypotheses.
- Withheld predictions.
- Unresolved portions.
- Evidence of prior exposure or recognition.

Do not request or require private chain-of-thought. Evaluation uses the final
report, predictions, and other observable, recoverable artifacts. Concise
reasons and evidence may be requested, but hidden reasoning is neither required
nor treated as missing trial data.

## Run and retry rules

Before a future trial begins, predeclare:

- Requested run count.
- Validity criteria.
- Retry conditions.
- Failure handling.
- Retention rule.

Do not retry merely because a model:

- Fails to decode.
- Produces an unexpected mapping.
- Criticizes the message.
- Reports indeterminacy.
- Rejects the premise.
- Reaches a lower decoder level than expected.

Preserve all valid and invalid runs. A retry must follow the frozen retry
conditions and must not become best-of-N selection, favorable-output
selection, or silent replacement. Failed, partial, indeterminate, and
unexpected decodes remain evidence about the exact tested conditions.

## Tool tracks

Keep these future trial tracks distinct:

- No-tool decoding.
- Calculator-enabled decoding.
- Code-execution decoding.
- Web-enabled decoding.
- Repository-exposed decoding.
- Human-assisted decoding.

Do not merge them silently. Record which tools were available, which were
used, tool outputs, retrieval sources, operator interventions, and any feature
that could not be disabled. Tool-enabled success does not transfer
automatically to a no-tool track.

## Scoring separation

A valid future scoring process requires:

- Decoder output freezes before solution reveal.
- The scorecard version freezes before execution.
- Scoring occurs against exact artifacts.
- Scorer corrections are logged.
- The signal designer's intended meaning is not automatically treated as the
  only plausible interpretation.
- Unexpected valid interpretations remain visible.

The attempt must not define or weaken its own test after results appear.
Scoring records must retain per-level evidence, counterevidence, confidence,
alternative interpretations, predictions, validity, and contamination status.
A finite trial result remains conditional on the exact signal, decoder,
context, tools, prompt, and protocol.

## Cost and authorization boundary

BSR-001 authorizes no paid model use, API use, token purchase, cloud
activation, or trial execution. It also authorizes no model selection, free
trial activation, billing setup, or consumption of paid credits. Future tests
may remain dormant until suitable access and a separate explicit authorization
exist.
