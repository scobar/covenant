# SDR-001 — Primary Framing-Locality Hypothesis

> **SDR-001 v0.1 — NONCANONICAL SUCCESSOR-DESIGN RESEARCH — NO SIG-002 ATTEMPT, STREAM, HOLDOUT, OR MODEL TRIAL**

## Primary design hypothesis

> Holding semantic content and framing-cue budget fixed, locally adjacent
> boundary-role cues may improve global framing recovery relative to globally
> concentrated framing cues.

## Null hypothesis

> The placement of framing cues produces no material framing-recovery
> difference under the frozen criteria.

## Regression hypothesis

> Local repetition may increase clutter, encourage incorrect local parsing, or
> degrade artificiality or numeracy.

## Manipulated and held-constant dimensions

- Manipulated variable: placement or locality of framing cues.
- Baseline family: framing information concentrated primarily in global
  headers, outer boundaries, and hierarchical delimiter placement.
- Treatment family: the same framing-cue multiset redistributed so equivalent
  boundary-role information recurs adjacent to the local units it frames.
- Held constant: cue-pattern multiset, total framing-event count, nonframing
  event bytes, semantic graph, truth assignments, example and query semantics,
  duplicate-body policy, neutral instruction, scoring logic, and other design
  dimensions as practical.

Changing cue count, cue strength, semantic content, arithmetic teaching,
grammar, query marking, event-class count, duplication, or provider together
with locality is confounded and unacceptable.

## Alternative explanations to preserve

- Cue strength rather than locality.
- Hidden parse complexity.
- Early hypothesis lock-in.
- Provider or hidden-backend behavior.
- Prior exposure or other contamination.
- Instruction or reporting strategy.
- Failure of surface novelty.
- Scorecard mismatch.

These hypotheses have no current comparative result label. SDR-001 creates no
finding and does not predict which condition will perform better.
