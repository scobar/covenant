# SDR-001 — Treatment-Family Research Space

> **SDR-001 v0.1 — NONCANONICAL SUCCESSOR-DESIGN RESEARCH — NO SIG-002 ATTEMPT, STREAM, HOLDOUT, OR MODEL TRIAL**

These six families are abstract implementation spaces. None is selected,
instantiated, preferred, or assigned an exact pattern.

## 1. Boundary-adjacent role recurrence

- **What changes:** Global framing instances would be repositioned so abstract
  boundary-role information occurs beside the unit it frames.
- **What remains constant:** Semantic source, cue-pattern multiset, framing
  event count, nonframing bytes, curriculum, and scoring.
- **How it could improve framing:** Nearby role evidence may reduce the memory
  distance between a boundary and its framed unit.
- **How it could mislead:** Repetition may be read as data, decoration, or an
  extra hierarchy level.
- **Confounds:** Added cues, changed cue strength, changed record count, or a
  new section grammar.
- **Cue-multiset equality:** Conceptually feasible only by relocating existing
  cue instances; a future exact proof is mandatory.
- **Parse uniqueness and carrier neutrality:** A unique intended parse may be
  preserved in a total-order abstract event model, but both claims require
  exact later proof and no carrier selection.

## 2. Nested local framing signatures

- **What changes:** Existing abstract hierarchy-role cues would recur at the
  local nesting depth they identify.
- **What remains constant:** Cue identities and multiplicities, total framing
  events, semantic graph, nonframing bytes, and all eighteen invariants.
- **How it could improve framing:** Local nesting signatures may make boundary
  order recoverable without relying only on distant global delimiters.
- **How it could mislead:** Similar signatures at adjacent depths may collapse
  roles or suggest false recursive content.
- **Confounds:** New signature types, extra depth, changed delimiter strength,
  or changed parse complexity.
- **Cue-multiset equality:** Feasible only if every signature is an existing
  cue instance redistributed one-for-one.
- **Parse uniqueness and carrier neutrality:** Must remain demonstrable in the
  abstract ordered stream; no physical realization is implied.

## 3. Section-local header echoes

- **What changes:** Existing abstract header-role cues would be relocated or
  echoed locally using the same pre-existing cue instances.
- **What remains constant:** Header semantics, section count and order, cue
  multiset, budget, nonframing bytes, examples, and queries.
- **How it could improve framing:** Local header-role evidence may connect a
  section identity with nearby section content.
- **How it could mislead:** An echo may be interpreted as content, a new
  section, or an unmatched start/end pair.
- **Confounds:** Introducing an additional header, changing sentinel strength,
  or altering section differentiation.
- **Cue-multiset equality:** Possible only through redistribution, never by
  adding or duplicating header cues.
- **Parse uniqueness and carrier neutrality:** Requires an exact later proof
  that the same abstract cue types retain one coherent parse without a carrier.

## 4. Record/block/section role echoes

- **What changes:** The placement of existing cues assigned to record, block,
  and section roles would be made more locally adjacent.
- **What remains constant:** Role inventory, role multiplicities, counts,
  semantic content, nonframing bytes, and scoring denominator.
- **How it could improve framing:** Recurrent local role context may clarify
  which hierarchy level a boundary serves.
- **How it could mislead:** Role echoes may be mistaken for additional records
  or may overemphasize local boundaries at the expense of global order.
- **Confounds:** Changing the number of records, blocks, sections, event
  classes, or framing roles.
- **Cue-multiset equality:** Conceptually feasible only under a one-for-one
  assignment proof across all hierarchy roles.
- **Parse uniqueness and carrier neutrality:** Must remain unique under the
  shared abstract event model and independent of a chosen carrier.

## 5. Locally repeated synchronization cues

- **What changes:** Existing synchronization-role cues would be distributed
  nearer to local restart or framing sites.
- **What remains constant:** The complete cue bag, framing-event budget,
  outer-boundary semantics, body duplication, and nonframing content.
- **How it could improve framing:** Local synchronization evidence may reduce
  dependence on an uninterrupted global parse.
- **How it could mislead:** Synchronization cues may be treated as resets,
  erasures, or independent messages.
- **Confounds:** Adding error correction, changing corruption assumptions,
  adding restarts, or increasing redundancy.
- **Cue-multiset equality:** Feasible only by relocation; no new redundancy is
  permitted in this locality experiment.
- **Parse uniqueness and carrier neutrality:** A future exact design must show
  one intended total-order parse without selecting timing or a carrier.

## 6. Matched-budget framing redistribution

- **What changes:** The entire existing framing-cue multiset would be assigned
  a different locality distribution under a formal matching constraint.
- **What remains constant:** Every cue pattern and multiplicity, total framing
  events, all nonframing bytes, semantic graph, curriculum, and scoring logic.
- **How it could improve framing:** A globally optimized redistribution may
  make roles locally legible while preserving total cue strength.
- **How it could mislead:** A different distribution may change parse
  complexity, create clustering, or weaken global coherence.
- **Confounds:** Any budget mismatch, changed stream length, new cue pattern,
  or result-dependent selection among redistributions.
- **Cue-multiset equality:** Central to the family and conceptually feasible,
  but exact equality must be mechanically proven.
- **Parse uniqueness and carrier neutrality:** Both remain mandatory and
  unproven until a later exact design is frozen.
