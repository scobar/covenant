# SIG-001 — Frozen Trial-Validity and Contamination Policy

> **FROZEN TRIAL-VALIDITY AND CONTAMINATION POLICY SIG-001-TV-0.1 — NO TRIAL EXECUTED**

## Freeze and Evidence Boundary

This policy freezes before the event stream. It cannot be weakened or revised
in response to the generated stream, decoder behavior, a trial outcome, or a
desired empirical result.

`Frozen` identifies exact historical trial rules. It does not mean SIG-001 is
decoded, successful, validated, accepted, canonical, or suitable for
transmission. No decoder, prompt, packet, trial, output, score, holdout, or
empirical decode rate exists.

The public solution remains administrator material that must be withheld from
an active decoder context until that decoder's output freezes.

## Protocol-Valid Future Trial Requirements

A future candidate trial is protocol-valid only if:

- It uses the exact frozen stream or a separately frozen authorized variant.
- The decoder output is frozen before solution exposure.
- The scorecard was frozen before the trial.
- No symbol mapping, grammar, relation key, query answer, desired decoder
  level, or target outcome is supplied.
- The decoder receives no BSR-001, SIG-001 design, solution, README, STATUS,
  or repository summary.
- Web and repository access are disabled where possible.
- Any unavailable controls are disclosed.
- Complete prompts, settings, tools, context, and output are preserved.
- The full output fits without unreported truncation.
- Retry and invalidity rules are fixed in advance.

The prohibition on supplying BSR-001, SIG-001 design, solution, README,
STATUS, or repository summary applies to material actively supplied in the
trial context. A decoder may nevertheless have prior exposure that the
administrator cannot erase. Such exposure must be classified below and limits
the evidence even when the active administration otherwise follows the
protocol.

The exact trial prompt and packet require later authorization and must freeze
before execution. A valid trial must not request private chain-of-thought;
observable final reports, predictions, and explanations are sufficient.

## Known Design-Exposed Entities

The following are known contaminated for blind exact-SIG-001 decoding:

- Scott Barbian.
- ChatGPT contexts involved in SIG-001 design.
- Codex contexts involved in SIG-001 construction or auditing.
- Any person or model shown the formal specification, scorecard answers,
  solution key, or repository before output freeze.

Design exposure cannot be cured merely by starting a new chat with the same
exposed person or by omitting disclosure from the trial record.

## Public-Attempt Contamination

- SIG-001 is public after commit.
- `No known exposure` will not prove absence of training or retrieval
  exposure.
- A model that recognizes the artifact must disclose that fact.
- Repository or exact-text lookup makes the trial exposed.
- Public SIG-001 trials are conditional evidence.
- A later sealed surface-novel variant is needed for stronger contamination
  control.

Public status does not make testing useless, but it limits what any result can
support. Recognition, retrieval, memorization, reconstruction, and mixed
routes must remain distinguishable where evidence permits.

## Allowed Validity Classifications

Use only:

- `Valid — no known exposure`
- `Valid but prior-exposed`
- `Valid but repository-exposed`
- `Contaminated`
- `Invalid — technical`
- `Invalid — protocol violation`

Apply the following mutually exclusive decision order:

1. `Invalid — protocol violation` if the active administration supplies a
   prohibited clue or project artifact, changes a frozen rule, edits output
   before freeze, selectively retains a run, or otherwise violates the frozen
   protocol. This classification takes priority over every exposure class.
2. `Invalid — technical` if a transport, artifact, truncation, preservation,
   or context failure prevents a complete substantive run and no protocol
   violation occurred.
3. `Contaminated` if the decoder is known to have received or retrieved the
   exact symbol mapping, grammar, relation key, Q1–Q7 answers, or administrator
   solution before its output froze, or explicitly recognizes and uses that
   solution. A contaminated run is not a valid trial.
4. `Valid but repository-exposed` if the run is protocol-valid and there is
   known prior or during-run exposure to the public repository or exact
   SIG-001 artifact, but no evidence meeting the `Contaminated` criterion.
5. `Valid but prior-exposed` if the run is protocol-valid and known
   non-repository prior exposure exists, but neither repository exposure nor
   solution exposure is known.
6. `Valid — no known exposure` if the run is protocol-valid and no prior,
   repository, exact-artifact, or solution exposure is known.

Repository exposure takes precedence over nonspecific prior exposure. Known
solution exposure takes precedence over both. Missing exposure evidence never
upgrades a run beyond what the preserved record supports.

`Valid — no known exposure` records available evidence only. It is not proof
that training, retrieval, memory, or indirect exposure is absent.

All three `Valid` classifications may enter an exposure-stratified valid-trial
denominator. They must be reported separately and must not be pooled into one
unqualified rate without a later explicit analysis. `Contaminated` and both
`Invalid` classifications are excluded from the valid-trial denominator but
remain preserved.

## Technical Invalidity

Examples that may support `Invalid — technical` are:

- Wrong or corrupted artifact supplied.
- Confirmed transport or provider failure before substantive decoding.
- Unrecoverable output truncation.
- Inability to preserve the complete output.
- A context limit that prevents receipt of the declared complete artifact.

Technical invalidity does not convert a failure or partial decode into a retry.

## Protocol Invalidity or Contamination

Examples include:

- Supplying a mapping, grammar, answer, target level, or desired result.
- Supplying project design or solution material before output freeze.
- Unreported repository, web, retrieval, or operator assistance.
- Editing decoder output before it freezes.
- Changing the scorecard, validity rule, or retry rule after execution begins.
- Selectively retaining only favorable runs.

Known prior or repository exposure is not by itself a protocol violation when
it was not actively supplied by the trial administration and is fully
disclosed. It instead receives the applicable `Valid but ...` classification,
unless exact solution exposure makes the run `Contaminated`.

The complete invalid or contaminated record must be preserved under any later
authorized trial protocol.

## Retry Rules

A future trial may retry only for:

- Transport or provider failure.
- Wrong artifact supplied.
- Unrecoverable truncation.
- Inability to preserve output.
- Confirmed prohibited-context contamination before substantive decoding.

Do not retry because of:

- Failure to decode.
- Partial decoding.
- Unexpected interpretation.
- Indeterminacy.
- Criticism.
- A lower level than hoped.
- An inconvenient result.

Retry eligibility, requested run count, and replacement rules must freeze
before execution. No best-of-N selection, favorable-output selection, or
silent replacement is permitted.

## Output Freeze and Scoring

For a later authorized trial:

1. Preserve the complete prompts, settings, tools, context, and artifact.
2. Preserve and freeze the complete decoder output.
3. Record validity and contamination evidence.
4. Only then reveal the solution to the scoring context.
5. Score against the already frozen scorecard.
6. Preserve unexpected interpretations and all corrections.

The designer's vocabulary is not automatically the only valid interpretation.
Predictive adequacy and internal coherence must be evaluated without changing
the pretrial criteria.

## No Trial Now

- No decoder is selected.
- No prompt is frozen.
- No trial packet exists.
- No run occurs.
- No output exists.
- No score exists.
- No empirical decode rate exists.

This policy authorizes no model use, human-decoder selection, holdout,
repository packet, carrier, distribution, or transmission.
